# Rendering And Shaders

## Purpose

This document defines the contracts between Three.js materials, shared uniforms, custom attributes, and GLSL replacements. Read it before changing a material, upgrading Three.js, or editing files under [`src/shaders/`](../src/shaders/).

## Scene Setup

[`src/renderSetup.js`](../src/renderSetup.js) (`RenderSetup`) owns the global rendering setup:

- `WebGLRenderer` is created with `antialias: false`, `depth: false`, and `powerPreference: 'high-performance'` (the discrete GPU on dual-GPU laptops): the scene always renders through the post-processing composer, whose 2x MSAA buffer is the only antialiasing, and the canvas receives only the finished full-screen composite.
- The depth buffer is the standard 24-bit one. A logarithmic depth buffer makes every material write `gl_FragDepth`, which disables early depth rejection (and hidden-surface removal on tile-based GPUs); dropping it cut the frame time by about 10% at `3840 × 2160` on an Apple M1.
- Renderer size follows the viewport. The pixel ratio is adaptive ([`src/adaptivePixelRatio.js`](../src/adaptivePixelRatio.js), `params.pixelRatio`): it starts at the display ratio capped at `2` and, over windows of `60` frames, steps down by `0.25` (to `min`, default `1.25`) while the mean frame interval is above `17.5` ms, which keeps 60 fps. After `3` windows below `16.9` ms it tries one step up; a try that does not stay clean for `2` windows is undone and doubles the wait, up to `120` windows (about two minutes), so a device at its limit settles with rare one-second dips. At a settled ratio, windows between `16.9` and `17.5` ms change nothing, so occasional hitches the resolution cannot fix keep it; intervals above `250` ms (shader compiles, a hidden tab) and the `10` frames after a change are skipped. Each change resizes the composer buffers. On a `3840 × 2160` headless-Chrome canvas with ACES on an Apple M1 (about `25` ms per frame at ratio `2`), it settled at `1.5` within two seconds and averaged `16.8` ms over a minute. `?dpr=<ratio>` pins the ratio (clamped to `0.5`–`2`) and turns adaptation off, for screenshots and benchmarks; the `?gui=1` **Performance** folder toggles **Adaptive resolution** and sets **Minimum pixel ratio** live. `getRenderStats().adaptivePixelRatio` reports `enabled`, `ratio`, `min`, `max`, `lastMeanMs`, `holdWindows`, and `changes`. The ratio cannot guarantee 60 fps on a device that misses it at the minimum.
- The perspective camera uses near/far planes of `1` and `10000`. With near `1`, one 24-bit depth step stays below `0.25` units out to the fog end (about `2000` units). Nothing comes within one unit of the chase camera; the `?debug=1` orbit camera (minimum distance `2`) can clip the airplane when zoomed fully in.
- Desktop starts at a `60` degree FOV and zoom `1`; mobile starts at `80` degrees and zoom `0.8`.
- The scene background and fog share the day/night horizon color, rewritten every frame by `DayNight`. Fog spans `params.fog.near` to `params.fog.far` (default `200` to `2100` world units), which `DayNight` copies into `scene.fog` every frame.
- Fog is radial: [`src/radialFog.js`](../src/radialFog.js), imported by `main.js` before any material compiles, replaces Three.js's `fog_vertex` depth (`-mvPosition.z`) with the distance from the eye (`length(mvPosition.xyz)`) in `ShaderChunk`, so every built-in material with fog uses it. With depth fog the curved edge was hazier at the center of the view than at its sides; radial fog keeps the haze uniform along the edge's arc. The module warns in the console if a Three.js upgrade changes the replaced line.
- An ambient light and two directional lights (sun and moon) are driven by the day/night cycle. `params.ambientLight`, `params.directionalLight` (sun), and `params.moonLight` (default `1.2`) are peak intensities that the cycle scales every frame. All three are sliders in the **Lights** GUI folder.
- Tone mapping defaults to ACES Filmic (`ACESFilmicToneMapping`) with sRGB output, since 2026-10-03; before, it was `NoToneMapping`, and the light and palette intensities were tuned that way, so ACES desaturates the day slightly, saturates dusk, and darkens the night. `params.toneMapping.mode` (a `THREE.*ToneMapping` constant) and the day exposure `params.toneMapping.exposure` (default `1`) are applied to `renderer.toneMapping` and `renderer.toneMappingExposure` before `PostProcessing` is created. From then on `DayNight` rewrites the exposure every frame: `lerp(exposure, nightExposure, night)`, where `night` is the cycle's night factor and `params.toneMapping.nightExposure` defaults to `1.3`, so the exposure is `1` by day, `1.3` at midnight, and blends with the keyframes in between (about `1.06` at sunrise and sunset, `1.21` at dusk, `1.24` before dawn), which partly offsets the darker ACES night. The **Tone mapping** GUI folder switches between None, Linear, Reinhard, Cineon, ACES Filmic, AgX, and Neutral and sets **Day exposure** and **Night exposure** (`0`–`4`, applied on the next frame; Three.js ignores both with None). A mode change (`RenderSetup.setToneMapping()`) calls `PostProcessing.updateToneMapping()`; Three.js recompiles the affected materials on their next draw. The composer renders the scene offscreen, where Three.js does not tone map, so the `ToneMappingEffect` below applies the operator.
- The camera is attached to `Plane`, so its transform is relative to the moving player object.

Resize changes the camera aspect and projection matrix, passes the new display maximum to the adaptive ratio, applies the current ratio, and then resizes the post-processing composer, which resizes the renderer and its buffers to the drawing-buffer size. The pixel ratio must be set before `PostProcessing.setSize()`.

## Post-Processing Pipeline

[`src/postProcessing.js`](../src/postProcessing.js) owns a `postprocessing` `EffectComposer` with 2x MSAA buffers (clamped to the device limit), a `RenderPass`, and one `EffectPass` containing `SpeedEffect`, `LensFlareEffect`, the optional `ToneMappingEffect`, and `FilmGrainEffect`, in that order. `World.tic()` calls `postProcessing.render(deltaTime)` instead of `renderer.render()`; the composer disables `renderer.autoClear` and clears through the render pass.

- **Always on:** every frame renders the scene into the offscreen buffer and composites it to the canvas in one effect pass; there is no bypass path. At intensity `0` the speed effect renders no blur level and its composite returns the input color, so the pass costs one full-screen draw. The canvas has no MSAA or depth buffer (see above), so the composer's 2x MSAA is the only antialiasing in every state.
- **Tone mapping:** the composer's offscreen render pass writes untone-mapped linear color. When `renderer.toneMapping` is not `NoToneMapping`, the `EffectPass` also holds a `postprocessing` `ToneMappingEffect` after `SpeedEffect`, with the matching mode (`TONE_MAPPING_MODES`) and the renderer exposure, and the composer buffers hold floats so values above `1` survive until tone mapping: `R11F_G11F_B10F` (`UnsignedInt101111Type` with `RGBFormat`, 4 bytes per sample) where the device renders it with 2x MSAA, otherwise `HalfFloatType` (`getSceneBufferFormat()`: `EXT_color_buffer_float` and `2` among the renderbuffer sample counts the context reports for that format). The packed format halves the multisampled store and resolve of RGBA16F, the largest cost of the chain, and has no alpha, which nothing reads: the canvas is opaque and the effects pass alpha through. Its 6-bit (red, green) and 5-bit (blue) mantissas showed no banding in the sky at day, dusk, or night. The composer's `createBuffer()` only sets the type, so `createComposer()` also sets the format of the input buffer and of its clone, the output buffer; the speed-effect levels take it in `initialize()`. With `NoToneMapping` the buffers stay 8-bit sRGB, as small. The half-float buffers cost about `23%` more frame time than None at `3840 × 2160` on an Apple M1 (alternated runs on a machine also running another Chrome tab, so approximate); 8-bit buffers took `18.7%` off the frame at `2400 × 1100` (`--dpr 1`) on 2026-10-12, and the packed format with the depth resolve below and the dark-light skip ([Curved-World Lighting](#curved-world-lighting)) `14`–`17.5%`. Passes cannot change frame-buffer type after initialization, so switching between None and a tone-mapped mode disposes and rebuilds the composer, render pass, effects, and effect pass (`createComposer()`); switching between two tone-mapped modes only changes the effect mode. `getPostProcessingStats()` reports `toneMapping`, `toneMappingExposure`, `frameBufferType`, `frameBufferFormat`, and `depthResolved` (this frame's depth resolve).
- **Scene depth:** `createComposer()` attaches a `DepthTexture` (default `UnsignedIntType`, the same 24-bit format as the depth renderbuffer it replaces) to the composer's input buffer, which the [lens flare](#lensflareeffect) reads. Three.js resolves the multisampled depth into that buffer when `resolveDepthBuffer` is set, so the texture costs no extra bandwidth. `PostProcessing.render()` sets it each frame from `LensFlareEffect.prepare()`, which decides before the render pass whether the flare shows: frames without the flare skip the depth resolve (about `4%` of the frame at `2400 × 1100`), and the texture then keeps a stale depth nothing reads; the composer's own depth texture (an effect with `EffectAttribute.DEPTH`) would switch both buffers to 32-bit float depth and copy it once more per frame. The render pass renders into the input buffer and the effect pass only reads it, so the texture is never sampled while written. This relies on the composer keeping its input buffer as the render pass target; `composer.dispose()` frees the texture.
- **Film grain:** `FilmGrainEffect` ([`film-grain-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/film-grain-fragment.glsl)) is the last effect of the same pass, so it adds no draw of its own.
  - The grain is static: a per-pixel hash of `gl_FragCoord` with no time input, so the pattern stays fixed on screen.
  - It declares `inputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, so `postprocessing` hands it the display-space (sRGB) color, after tone mapping. It scales that value by a factor in `[1 − intensity, 1 + intensity]`, the same result as a `2 × src × dst` multiply overlay on the canvas.
  - `params.postProcessing.grain.intensity` (default `0.035`) is read every frame; `0` leaves the color unchanged.
  - The grain has no texture reads and no offscreen target.
- **MSAA cost:** without `WEBGL_multisampled_render_to_texture` (absent in desktop Chrome on Apple M1), the composer writes its whole multisampled scene target to memory and resolves it with a blit. This is the largest cost of the chain, larger than blur and aberration together (see `FEAT-001` in [Roadmap](ROADMAP.md)). The composer therefore uses 2x MSAA instead of 4x.
- **Intensity:**
  - `World.tic()` passes the larger of the positive visual speed effect (`uAcceleration`, `0` during the debug pause) and the GUI `params.speedEffect`.
  - `setSpeedEffect()` first remaps that value from `0 → 1` onto `idle → 1`, with `idle = params.postProcessing.idleSpeedEffect` (default `0.3`). It then maps the result through `smoothstep(0.1, 1)` and raises it to at least `params.postProcessing.preview`.
  - At cruise, while braking, and during the debug pause, the edges therefore keep the blur and aberration of a `0.3` speed effect (intensity about `0.126`, two blur levels on a desktop canvas; the former `0.4`, intensity about `0.259`, needed four, and the speed effect at rest cost about `13%` of the frame at `2400 × 1100`). A boost animates the rest up to full strength.
  - The remap affects only the post-processing. `FollowCamera` drives the camera FOV and distance kick from the same visual speed effect without it, so cruise keeps the base FOV and camera distance.
- **Future passes:** add them to `PostProcessing`, preferably as effects in the existing `EffectPass` so they merge into its single full-screen draw.

### `SpeedEffect`

[`src/speedEffect.js`](../src/speedEffect.js) and [`speed-effect.glsl`](../src/shaders/speed-effect.glsl) combine an edge blur and radial chromatic aberration in one fullscreen effect.

- In `Effect.update()`, a private `BlurPyramidPass` downsamples the scene buffer into up to four successively half-resolution render targets (level 1 at half resolution) with a 5-tap dual filter ([`blur-downsample-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/blur-downsample-fragment.glsl)): a bilinear center tap weighted `4/8` and four diagonal bilinear taps one source texel away weighted `1/8` each, a tent over about 4x4 source texels. Only the levels the current maximum blur reaches are rendered (`getRequiredBlurLevels()`): the mask peaks in the viewport's corners, and a corner that needs up to `2^0.15` (about 11%) more than a level's blur saturates at that level instead of rendering one more, so it may get slightly less blur than asked; any blur at all renders at least one level, since the composite then reads the pyramid. Each level is cleared before it is drawn, so a tiled GPU does not load the previous frame's contents. Four levels cap the blur at about `16` pixels: wherever `strength * mask * height` exceeds that, the blur saturates instead of growing. The level-1 pass reads the full-resolution buffer and is about three quarters of the pyramid cost; the capped levels were below 2%. The level targets inherit the composer frame-buffer type and format and, for 8-bit buffers, sRGB storage.
- Each pixel's blur radius becomes a fractional level `lod = log2(pixels)`; level `k` blurs by about `2^k` pixels and level `0` is the full-resolution input. A private `BlurUpsamplePass` then folds the pyramid back up from the coarsest rendered level to level `1`, at each level's resolution: `U(k) = mix(D(k), U(k + 1), clamp(lod - k, 0, 1))`, where the coarsest `U` is its downsample `D` ([`blur-upsample-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/blur-upsample-fragment.glsl)). Each pixel therefore blends its two nearest levels before full resolution, and a `lod` above the rendered levels saturates at the coarsest one.
- The full-resolution composite reads only `U(1)` (the `blurBuffer` uniform, or `D(1)` when a single level is rendered) and mixes it with the sharp input by `clamp(lod, 0, 1)`. Upsampled levels and the composite use a four-tap cubic B-spline reconstruction ([`bspline.glsl`](../src/shaders/bspline.glsl)) to avoid bilinear blockiness, so each blurred channel costs 4 taps, plus the input where `lod < 1`.
- Chromatic aberration samples red outward, green in place, and blue inward along the direction from the center, all at the same blur level, with the same B-spline reconstruction: at most 12 blur taps per aberrated pixel instead of the 24 of a two-level blend in the composite. It is skipped below half a pixel of dispersion. Pixels with neither effect return the input color without sampling.
- Each effect has `strength`, `start`, `end`, and `curve` in `params.postProcessing`. The mask is `intensity * pow(smoothstep(start, end, radius), curve)`. Radius is `length(vec2(x, y * verticalScale)) * √2`, measured from the viewport center in UV. It is `0` at the center and about `0.71` at the left and right edge midpoints, and never changes horizontally. `params.postProcessing.verticalScale` (default `0.78`, shared by both effects) scales only the vertical component: the top and bottom edge midpoints reach about `0.71 * verticalScale` and the corners reach `√(0.5 + 0.5 * verticalScale²)`. With `1` the falloff is circular (`1` at the corners), and with `0` the effect depends only on horizontal distance. Blur strength is a fraction of viewport height; aberration strength is a UV offset at the edges.
- The radius, mask, and `lod` live in [`speed-effect-mask.glsl`](../src/shaders/speed-effect-mask.glsl), included by the composite and the upsample shader through `vite-plugin-glsl`. Both passes share the same `intensity`, `blurParams`, and `verticalScale` `Uniform` objects, so they always agree on each pixel's level. `BLUR_LEVELS` exists only in JavaScript; the shaders have no per-level samplers.

### `LensFlareEffect`

[`src/lensFlareEffect.js`](../src/lensFlareEffect.js) draws the sun's lens flare; the pure rules and defaults are in [`src/lensFlarePolicy.js`](../src/lensFlarePolicy.js) (`createLensFlareSettings()`, `params.postProcessing.lensFlare`).

- **Sun:** `World.tic()` calls `PostProcessing.setSun(dayNightState)` before `render()`. It keeps the direction, the linear sun color, and the sky disc's visibility at the current horizon dip (`getSunDiscVisibility()`, the curve of `sky.frag.glsl`) in an object that survives composer rebuilds.
- **Gate:** in `prepare()`, which `PostProcessing.render()` calls before the render pass (so the scene depth is resolved only when the flare shows), the effect brings the camera's world matrix up to date and projects the direction (`w = 0`) through the camera's current view and projection matrices (`getSunScreenPosition()`) and sizes the occlusion disc from the projection's scale (`getOcclusionRadiusUv()`, so zoom and aspect apply; `occlusionRadius` `1.3°`, the sky disc is `1.1°`–`1.4°`). The gate (`getLensFlareGate()`) is `0` while disabled, at zero intensity, or with the sun behind the camera; otherwise it is the disc visibility (so the flare follows the disc behind the curved horizon and is off at night) times the edge fade (`getScreenEdgeFade()`: `1` while the disc is `edgeMargin`, `0.06` UV, inside the screen, `0` once it has left it). `Effect.update()` reads that gate after the render pass. At gate `0` nothing else runs.
- **Occlusion:** a private `VisibilityPass` renders a single texel from the [scene depth](#post-processing-pipeline): `32` taps on a golden-angle disc over the sun ([`lens-flare-taps.glsl`](../src/shaders/lens-flare-taps.glsl), [`lens-flare-visibility-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/lens-flare-visibility-fragment.glsl)) count the share left on the sky. The sky dome writes no depth, so its pixels keep the cleared `1.0`; everything drawn (terrain, trees, clouds, boats, the airplane) lies below the `0.99999` threshold, while the trails and the aurora, which write no depth, never occlude. Taps off the screen are not counted. The count is blended with the other texel of a ping-pong pair by `exp(−dt / smoothing)` (`0.08` s), so dithered edges and the propeller never flicker the flare; a frame with the gate closed restarts the history. The texels are half float where the device renders it, 8-bit otherwise (where the easing stops a step short of its target).
- **Composite:** [`lens-flare-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/lens-flare-fragment.glsl) adds, to the linear color before tone mapping, `flareColor` (the sun's color × `intensity` × gate) × the visible share × four elements, sized in screen heights:
  - **Glow:** `intensity × (1 + d / size)^−falloff` around the sun (`0.25`, `0.08`, `2`).
  - **Starburst:** `rays` (`12`) rays fixed to the lens, alternately long and short, `pow(|cos|, sharpness)` wide (`30`), fading over `size` (`0.18`), turned by `rotation` (`0.2` rad), at `0.12`.
  - **Ghosts:** five soft tinted discs with a brighter rim along the line from the sun through the screen center (at `0.45`, `0.8`, `1.25`, `1.6`, and `2.1` of it, times `spacing`), sized from `size` (`0.05`), at `0.06`; they fade out as the sun nears the center.
  - **Halo:** a ring of `radius` (`0.42`) and `width` (`0.03`) around the center, its channels at slightly different radii, brightest toward the sun, at `0.04`.

  A uniform branch returns the input color while the gate is closed. **Debug > Show occlusion taps** paints each tap green over the sky and red where occluded.
- **Order:** the effect has no attributes, so the pass keeps it after `SpeedEffect` (whose blur and aberration leave the flare sharp) and before tone mapping and grain.
- **Stats:** `getPostProcessingStats().lensFlare` reports `enabled`, `gate`, `inFront`, `screen` (UV), `discVisibility`, `edgeFade`, `visibility` (the visible share, read back without stalling at most every `250` ms while stats are requested), and `taps`.
- **GUI:** the **Lens flare** folder sets everything live, since the effect reads its settings every frame: **Enabled**, **Intensity**, **Smoothing**, **Occlusion radius**, **Edge margin**, then **Glow**, **Starburst**, **Ghosts**, **Halo**, and **Debug** with the taps and readouts of the gate, the visible share, and the sun's screen position. The readouts ask the current effect, so they survive the composer rebuild of a tone mapping change.
- **Known limits:** the occlusion knows only depth, so the trails and the aurora never dim the flare. The intensities are tuned for ACES; with `NoToneMapping` the 8-bit buffers clamp the flare. Whether the depth arrives on devices with `WEBGL_multisampled_render_to_texture` is untested (the taps debug view shows it at once); `EffectAttribute.DEPTH` is the fallback.

## Day/Night Cycle

[`src/dayNight.js`](../src/dayNight.js) owns the sky dome and applies the cycle state. [`src/dayNightPolicy.js`](../src/dayNightPolicy.js) computes that state as pure data (sRGB triplets and scalars) without Three.js.

- **Time convention:** `params.dayNight.timeOfDay` is in `[0, 1)`: `0` midnight, `0.5` noon; on a flat horizon `0.25` is sunrise and `0.75` sunset. It advances by `deltaTime / cycleDuration` (default `360` seconds) unless `paused` is set; `?gui=1` sets it at startup. `?time=<0..1>` sets the starting value; without it the cycle starts at the user's local clock time (`getLocalTimeOfDay()`, seconds since local midnight over `86400`), or at a fixed `0.3` with `?debug=1` or `?gui=1` (`debugTimeOfDay`; `getStartTimeOfDay()` picks), then runs at its own pace, so it drifts from the real clock at once.
- **Curved horizon:** the terrain shader bends the world onto a sphere of radius `CURVATURE` (`3000`) centered below the plane (see [Curved-World Lighting](#curved-world-lighting)). From camera height `h` above sea level, the visible edge of the world sits `dip = acos(R / (R + h))` below the horizontal (about `0.21` rad at cruise height). `DayNight` recomputes the dip every frame from the camera's world Y, including the debug orbit camera. The **apparent elevation** `asin(direction.y) + dip` is the single reference for the sky gradient, disc visibility, palette timing, and light fades.
- **Palette time:** keyframes are authored for a flat horizon. `getPaletteTime()` computes the apparent sunrise and sunset (`0.25 ∓ δ`, `0.75 ± δ`, with `δ = asin(sin(dip) / cos(tilt)) / 2π`) and stretches that apparent day onto `[0.25, 0.75]` and the night onto the rest. The mapping is piecewise linear, continuous across midnight, monotonic, and the identity when `dip = 0`, so sunrise and sunset colors coincide with the sun meeting the curved edge at any altitude.
- **Keyframes:** `DAY_NIGHT_DEFAULTS.keyframes` (each with a `name` and `t`) interpolates zenith, horizon, sun and moon color and intensity, ambient color and intensity, the atmosphere ceiling, trail tint, star visibility, and a `night` factor (which blends the tone-mapping exposure from day to night value) with smoothstep blends that wrap across midnight. The `t = 0.8` dusk keyframe keeps the original static sky (fog `#191362`, linear atmosphere `(0.1, 0.015, 0.02)`), but with cool moonlight. At night the moon keyframes stay at full `params.moonLight`, and ambient light drops to `0.06` (midnight) to `0.15` (dusk) of `params.ambientLight`. The resulting direct-to-ambient ratio of about `13:1` keeps terrain relief readable under moonlight.
- **Live palette:** `params.dayNight.keyframes` is a mutable copy from `createDayNightPalette()`; `DayNight` interpolates it instead of the frozen defaults, so GUI edits apply on the next frame. `copyKeyframe()` writes a keyframe in place (used to reset), and `getTimeOfDayForPaletteTime()` inverts the palette-time mapping so `DayNight.previewKeyframe()` can pause the cycle exactly on a keyframe at the current dip. Keyframe times are not editable, which keeps them ordered.
- **Celestial arc and lights:** the sun rises at +X, sets at -X, and is tilted `0.5` radians toward +Z; the moon is always opposite. Each body drives its own `DirectionalLight`, whose intensity fades in over apparent elevation `[-0.03, 0.05]` rad. In the twilight band where `|asin(y)| < dip`, both are lit, as seen from altitude. No light switches direction. The lights cast no Three.js shadows (`renderer.shadowMap` stays off); only their position (direction) matters. Scenery shadows are a separate custom pass that follows the higher of the two lights (see [Scenery Shadows](#scenery-shadows)).
- **Sky dome:** a `SphereGeometry` of radius `5000` (inside the camera far plane) with a `ShaderMaterial` ([`sky.vert.glsl`](../src/shaders/sky.vert.glsl), [`sky.frag.glsl`](../src/shaders/sky.frag.glsl)), `BackSide`, no depth write, no fog, `renderOrder` `10`, and frustum culling disabled. It follows the camera's world position every frame. It draws after every opaque mesh with the depth test on, and its vertex shader moves it just inside the far plane (`z = 0.999999 w`), so only pixels the scene leaves empty run the sky shader; samples that alpha-to-coverage impostors leave uncovered keep the cleared depth and still get the sky. The gradient is a function of the elevation above the dipped horizon, `e = asin(direction.y) + uHorizonDipAngle`, where `DayNight` uploads the dip in radians (`uHorizonDipAngle`) and as `uHorizonDip = sin(dip)` for the disc fades: the color is `mix(horizon, zenith, 1 - exp(-e / uGradientHeight))`, and the horizon color is kept below the edge, so fogged terrain and sky meet without a seam. `uGradientHeight` comes from `params.dayNight.skyGradientHeight` (default `DAY_NIGHT_DEFAULTS.skyGradientHeight`, `0.095` rad). The exponential falloff concentrates the change near the edge, where lines of equal elevation still follow its arc on screen; a wide gradient reads as flat horizontal bands above the curved edge. It draws a sun disc (about `1.3°` radius) and halo, and a moon disc (about `0.9°` radius) and halo, both faded by apparent elevation; terrain hides them once they pass behind the curved edge. Hashed twinkling stars are drawn only while `uStarVisibility > 0`.
- **Per-frame writes:** fog color and range, background, sun and moon light colors, intensities and directions, ambient light, `uAtmosphere`, the shared `uSunDirection` (the sky dome's `uSunDirection` is the same wrapper), and the sky uniforms. Colors go through `Color.setRGB(..., SRGBColorSpace)`, and the state object and colors are reused without per-frame allocation.

## Shader Injection Pattern

Scene materials other than the sky dome and the aurora do not use `ShaderMaterial`. They start with built-in Three.js materials and patch generated shaders through `material.onBeforeCompile`. Every patch goes through `replaceChunks(source, { chunkName: replacement })` ([`src/shaderChunks.js`](../src/shaderChunks.js)), which throws when an `#include <chunkName>` is missing, so a Three.js upgrade that renames or drops a chunk fails at compile time instead of silently skipping the patch; [`test/shaderPatches.test.js`](../test/shaderPatches.test.js) applies every patch (terrain, scenery and cloud impostors with their wireframe twins, near scenery and cloud meshes, trail, propeller, debug markers) to the installed `ShaderLib` sources, and checks that the aurora's, the lens flare's, and the low-sun height map caster's materials supply every uniform their shaders declare. The defensive `ShaderChunk` edits in `radialFog.js` and `curvedLights.js` keep their own warnings. The sky dome and the aurora (both unlit and fog-free), the post-processing blur downsample and upsample, the two transient impostor bake passes, the two depth-only scenery shadow casters, the cloud shadow coverage caster and blur, the sea foam footprint caster, the lens flare's occlusion pass, and the low-sun height map caster are the only standalone `ShaderMaterial` instances.

## Shared GLSL Contract

[`src/shaders/common.glsl`](../src/shaders/common.glsl) retains Three.js's original `#include <common>` and adds:

- `uTime`: elapsed render time.
- `uCamera`: the plane's world position, used as the visual reference point.
- `uCurvature`: radius used by the curved-world projection, and `getCurvatureDrop()` from [`curvature-drop.glsl`](../src/shaders/curvature-drop.glsl), the drop at a distance from the eye (twin of `getCurvatureDrop()` in `chunkPolicy.js`).
- `uBiomeOffset`: the seeded `vec4` offset of the biome fields, from `createBiomeOffset()` in [`src/biome.js`](../src/biome.js): `xy` shifts world XZ for the climate, the rocky coast mask, and the color and palette noises; `zw` shifts the ice field in its noise space (the deep ocean field shares it, through `uBiomeOceanOffset`). The CPU placement code applies the same offset.
- `uAtmosphere`: the day/night color ceiling that distant terrain and scenery are clamped toward (`min(uAtmosphere, color)`) before fog. It replaces the former hard-coded dark maroon. Clouds skip the clamp (`SCENERY_NO_ATMOSPHERE_CLAMP`): it would tint their light albedo cyan by day, so only fog fades them.
- `wPosition`: world-space position passed from vertex to fragment stages.
- `distanceFromCamera`: distance used for curvature, scaling, atmospheric darkening, and fading.
- Shared `rotateAroundAxis()` (Rodrigues rotation used for bent normals) and simplex-noise helpers.

Helpers used by several chunks live in their own files, included with `vite-plugin-glsl` file includes and guarded by `#ifndef`, because one program can include them twice: [`curvature-drop.glsl`](../src/shaders/curvature-drop.glsl), [`curvature-bend.glsl`](../src/shaders/curvature-bend.glsl) (`getCurvatureBend()`: the drop and the rigid tilt with the sphere at a flat base, for the scenery instances and the aurora), [`rotate-yaw.glsl`](../src/shaders/rotate-yaw.glsl) (instance yaw), [`interleaved-gradient-noise.glsl`](../src/shaders/interleaved-gradient-noise.glsl) (dither and shadow rotation), [`biome-value.glsl`](../src/shaders/biome-value.glsl) (the biome fields, for the terrain's vertex and fragment stages), [`sea-ice-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-ice-pars.glsl) (the frozen sea's uniforms and shelf, for both stages), [`sea-surface-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-surface-pars.glsl) (the moving sea's waves and regions, for both terrain stages and the boats), and [`impostor-octahedral.glsl`](../src/shaders/impostor-octahedral.glsl), whose `selectImpostorFrames()` picks the three frames and weights for both the impostor and the shadow caster. sRGB conversions use three's `sRGBTransferOETF()` and `sRGBTransferEOTF()`. Constants the CPU also reads come from JavaScript as material defines, never as GLSL literals: the terrain bands and biome layers (`TERRAIN_SHADER_DEFINES`), the impostor layouts, and the shadow tap counts.

The terrain geometry also provides a custom scalar `height` attribute. Its value is the raw procedural height, including underwater values that differ from the CPU-clamped visible vertex position. Position, normal, and height arrays are generated in a worker, transferred, and wrapped in `BufferGeometry` on the main thread, where every chunk at one LOD shares one index and one uv attribute; the uv only feeds the normal map's tangent frame (see [Terrain](TERRAIN.md#height-generation)).

The plane trail uses dynamic world-space positions, a per-vertex `trailWidths` vector containing separate left and right widths, a `trailDistance` attribute recording distance traveled along the flight path, and a `trailBank` attribute recording the airplane's absolute roll at emission normalized against `PI / 4`. `WingTrails` ([`src/wingTrails.js`](../src/wingTrails.js)) updates these attributes every frame from the recent flight poses in `TrailHistory` ([`src/trailHistory.js`](../src/trailHistory.js)); the mesh is attached to the scene rather than the airplane. Each pose is emitted at the model's `trailAnchor` (`AIRPLANE_MODELS`), the wings' trailing edge at the tips in the airplane model's geometry units, on the upper wing for the biplane (see [Assets](ASSETS.md#airplane)), transformed by the model's world matrix so it follows pitch and roll, and the ribbon spans the model's rolled wing axis. Its default `9.6`-unit-wide ribbon uses `30` lengthwise segments; with the default line, edge, and border widths, the stripe centers sit about `0.97` units inside the ribbon edges, at about the `±3.8`-unit wing tips. Actual speed above cruise and path curvature widen the stripes; the outer wing is wider in a turn. The turn contribution starts at normalized curvature `0.65` and caps at `0.75` width, while maximum boosted speed can reach `1`. The trail shader passes UV, both widths, traveled distance, and bank to the fragment stage. Each stripe tapers from zero at the wing, broadens near the middle, and tapers to zero at the tail. Its centerline offset mixes a traveled-distance sine wave with a smaller simplex-noise variation; a side-specific offset derived from UV gives the two stripes different patterns. Oscillation amplitude is multiplied by the recorded bank, so it is zero for level wings and reaches the GUI value at maximum roll; it also rises from zero at the wing to full strength about `21` world units behind it. Simplex noise in trail space roughens both boundaries. Its coordinate is the traveled distance along the path, scaled by `0.5`, plus a side offset. The pattern therefore stays attached to emitted sections and keeps the same frequency in turns and on any heading. Noise amplitude follows the square of the width taper so the edges become progressively smoother toward both ends. Fragments outside the white core and its narrower black outline are discarded. The white core and dark outline have full opacity above `2.5` projected pixels; below that width their opacity eases down to `0.65`, without a hard visibility cutoff.

`params.trails` (`createAppParams()`) is the live tuning source. Ribbon width controls the CPU geometry span and the shader's world-unit-to-UV conversion. Line width, border width, edge amplitudes, and oscillation amplitude are world units. Edge frequencies scale the trail-space noise coordinate, which is half the traveled distance; oscillation frequency is cycles per world unit traveled. The debug GUI (`?gui=1`) updates all nine values without recompiling the material. The stripe center starts at its ribbon-relative offset, can move inward when larger widths require clipping protection, and then receives the oscillation offset.

## Shader Paths

### Terrain

- [`project-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/project-vertex.glsl) moves the sea's vertices with its waves near the airplane ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)), then applies distance-based curvature and the final projection. It also evaluates the ice field (`getIceValue()`, two `snoise`) per vertex into the varying `vIceValue`: its layers are broad (the finest about `670` units at the default size), so interpolating it across triangles moves the ice border by under two units even at the coarsest LOD, and the fragment needs no noise for it. The deep ocean field, as broad, follows the same way (`getOceanValue()`, two more `snoise`, already cut by the ice) into `vOceanValue`, which the land branch reads for the islets' biome and the sea for the deep ocean's settings. So does the climate (`getClimateNoise()`, three `snoise`) into `vClimateNoise`, except where the vertex lies deeper inside the ice or the deep ocean than the field's slope bound times the longest triangle edge (`uBiomeIce.w` or `uBiomeOcean.z` times `uBiomeClimate.w`, [Terrain](TERRAIN.md#biome-field)): no pixel of its triangles reads the climate there, so it skips the noise (an `if`, which compilers keep as a branch, where a conditional expression may evaluate both sides). The sea state and the ripples' phase noise follow as varyings too (`vSeaState`, `vSeaRippleNoise`). The waves stop under the [frozen sea](#sea-ice) (`getSeaIceStill()`).
- [`color-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/color-fragment.glsl) keeps five elevation-based land bands and colors them with the palette of the biome under each pixel (see [Biome Field](TERRAIN.md#biome-field)). Above the sand level it reads the climate interpolated from the vertices (`vClimateNoise`, outside the ice and the deep ocean; per pixel it cost about `9.5%` of the frame over land at `2400 × 1100`, [`PERF-005`](ROADMAP.md#perf-005-gpu-frame-cost-at-120-hz)) and picks the biome with `getBiome()` from [`biome-value.glsl`](../src/shaders/biome-value.glsl), included by [`terrain-bands-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/terrain-bands-pars.glsl); the layers are the `BIOME_CLIMATE_LAYER_*`, `BIOME_ICE_LAYER_*`, and `BIOME_OCEAN_LAYER_*` defines from [`src/terrainBands.js`](../src/terrainBands.js), and the sizes, desert bias, ice and deep ocean thresholds, and forest ring are the `uBiomeClimate`, `uBiomeIce`, and `uBiomeOcean` uniforms (the deep ocean's noise offset is `uBiomeOceanOffset`). The band borders come from the same module: `TERRAIN_SAND_LEVEL` and, per band, `TERRAIN_<BAND>_WAVE`, `_LINE`, and `_LEVEL`, compared with `getTerrainBandHeight()` (see [Terrain Bands](TERRAIN.md#terrain-bands)).
- **Palettes:** `getTerrainColor(biome, band, variation)` reads `uTerrainColors[biome * 5 + band]` (sand, grass, land, rocks, snow) and drifts it toward `uTerrainVariations[...]` (color and amount) by the slow biome variation noise (two fields of the [noise tile](#noise-tile) at `0.001` and `0.01` per unit of the biome coordinates, sampled only for a biome with a varying band: the forest's grass, land, and rocks). `uTerrainStyles[biome]` scales the color noise patches (below) and sets the darkness of the band ink lines (`1` black, `0` none). `updateTerrainPaletteUniforms()` writes them from `params.terrainPalette` ([`src/terrainPalettePolicy.js`](../src/terrainPalettePolicy.js)): the forest and desert reproduce the former constants (forest grass, land, and rocks `#6d976d`, `#455f0c`, `#b66635`); the ice runs from a white shore (`#ffffff`) through a very pale blue plain (grass `#d1f1ff`) and light blue slopes (land `#8fd4ff`) to blue ice rock (`#70baff`) and pale snowy peaks (`#dbeeff`), with the light patches at `0.3`; the deep ocean's islets have pale yellow sand (`#fff6bd`), peach grass (`#ffb98a`), and green land (`#3e9c56`), with the light patches at `0.6` (their rocks and snow colors stay unused: the islets never reach those bands). The **Biomes > Forest / Desert / Ice / Deep ocean > Terrain palette** GUI folders edit them live.
- **Separators:** an antialiased black line hides the hard palette transitions on land only, leaving water colors and wave highlights independent: along the climate border outside the ice and the deep ocean, and along the ice border. The deep ocean's border needs none, because its slope sinks every land before it ([Terrain](TERRAIN.md#deep-ocean)). It has a constant world-space width (`BIOME_LINE_HALF_WIDTH`, `0.6` units on each side), so it keeps its size on the ground at any distance: `getBiomeSeparator()` divides the field by its gradient. The climate and ice fields are interpolated from the vertices, as is `wPosition.xz`, both affine across a triangle, so `getFieldGradient()` solves the interpolated field's world gradient exactly from their screen derivatives (`dFdx`/`dFdy` of `vClimateNoise` and `vIceValue`, taken with the ground's in uniform control flow), with no noise. Where the pixel's ground axes are nearly parallel (their sine below `BIOME_GRADIENT_MIN_SINE`, `0.02`: a cliff seen head-on), whose solution the derivatives' precision would spoil, it falls back to world-space differences of the exact fields (`getClimateGradient()`, forward, `0.5` units, three `snoise` per sample; `getIceGradient()`, central, `4` units). Along the forest ring the effective climate follows the ice field, so its gradient does too. The separator only runs where the field is within `(BIOME_LINE_HALF_WIDTH + 2 × pixel size)` times its gradient bound (`uBiomeClimate.z`, `uBiomeIce.w`, from `getBiomeGradientBounds()`). The antialiasing edge is the pixel's ground footprint along the gradient, from `dFdx`/`dFdy` of `wPosition.xz` taken in uniform control flow, so far away the line becomes thinner than a pixel and fades.
- **Shoreline:** raw heights from `0` to the sand level stay a black ink line, except in the ice, where they take the ice sand color: the ice never meets the water with a dark line.
- **Sea colors:** the shallow, open, deep, and abyss sea colors are `uSeaColors` (`params.terrainPalette.sea`, **Biomes > Sea**), by default `#88fbe4`, `#368eba`, `#04578b`, and `#002f61`. The abyss color fades in between the depths of `uSeaAbyssDepth` (`params.terrainPalette.abyssDepth`, `45` to `125` by default, **Abyss from** and **Abyss full at**), over the deep sea color. The rest of the sea never gets deeper than about `-53`, where the abyss is only `3%` of the color, so the abyss belongs to the [deep ocean](TERRAIN.md#deep-ocean) and its slope (its floor at `-110` takes `91%` of it), and sea pixels still evaluate no biome.
- **Color noise:** after the five bands, `color-fragment.glsl` multiplies land colors (height above `0.1`, so not the sea) by `getTerrainColorShade()` from [`terrain-color-noise-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/terrain-color-noise-pars.glsl). It samples two octaves from two fields of the [noise tile](#noise-tile) at the seeded biome coordinates (`wPosition.xz + uBiomeOffset`) times `uColorNoiseFrequency` (and `2.7` times it), with the pixel's ground size for the mip level, maps them to about `[0, 1]`, and brightens by up to `1 + uColorNoiseIntensity` through `smoothstep(threshold - softness, threshold + softness, noise)`, so patches have soft edges and cross band borders. The factor multiplies the color, so hues stay and the black band lines stay black. Higher `uColorNoiseThreshold` leaves fewer, smaller patches. The two octaves drift with `uTime * uColorNoiseSpeed` (noise units per second) in different directions and at different rates, so the patches slowly change shape rather than slide; `0` freezes them. `params.terrainColorNoise` (`createAppParams()`) sets the five uniforms (defaults: frequency `0.012`, intensity `0.75`, threshold `0.61`, softness `0.3`, speed `0.25`), and the **Terrain > Color noise** GUI folder updates them live without recompiling. Each biome scales the brightening by its palette's `colorNoise` (forest and desert `1`, ice `0.3`); at `0` the noise is not evaluated. The shade is applied before the biome separator, wave highlights, and atmosphere clamp; scenery impostors are not affected.
- **Rocky coast sand:** before the bands, outside the ice and the deep ocean (whose islets keep bright beaches), the sand color is multiplied by `mix(1, uCoastSandShade, getCoastRockMask(biomeXZ))`, so it darkens gradually on [rocky coast](TERRAIN.md#rocky-coast), without a border line. The mask (two `snoise()`, with the settings in `uCoastRockNoise` and `uCoastRockEdge`) is evaluated only where the height is below `TERRAIN_GRASS_LEVEL` plus the grass wave's reach, where sand can still show. `params.coastSand.shade` (`0.82`) sets the uniform, and **Terrain > Coast > Sand shade** updates it live.
- **Sea waves and foam lines:** after the bands, the sea's waves ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)) draw their crest lines and keep their normal for the normal map. Then the foam lines of the sea are `getSeaRipple(seaHeight, xz, oceanMask, state, pixel)` from [`sea-ripple-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-ripple-pars-fragment.glsl): animated lines within a depth band (from about `10` to `17` units deep for the sea and `3.5` to `7.5` for the deep ocean by default) that move toward the coast, wobble, and break into dashes, with the settings of the sea type under the pixel, scaled by the sea state. `seaHeight` is `getSeaFoamHeight()`: the raw height, so the lines follow the depth contours, fused near the sea rocks with the distance read from the [sea foam](#sea-foam) map. The lines are contours of that single height, so those of the coast and of the rocks merge and never cross. The line is mixed toward the foam color (`uSeaFoamColor`), and the [sea ice](#sea-ice) coverage removes it.
- `color-fragment.glsl` also writes `terrainBand`, the layer index read by the normal map: `0` sea (height at or below `0.1`), then `1` sand, `2` grass, `3` land, `4` rocks, `5` snow. It uses the same band defines and priority as the color bands, with the `TERRAIN_BAND_*` index defines, so every biome shares the five land layers; the frozen sea takes the snow layer.
- **Land branch:** below `TERRAIN_SAND_LEVEL` (`0.1`) no band, biome color, separator, or color noise reaches the output, so everything after the sea colors runs inside `if (wPosition.y >= TERRAIN_SAND_LEVEL)`. Sea pixels, most of the world, evaluate no biome noise at all (the ice field comes from the vertices); the band factors default to `1.0`, their value below every band. Only the position derivatives stay outside the branch, because derivatives need uniform control flow. Measured with `pnpm bench` at the former biome layout, the three-biome shader costs the same as the two-biome one (`+0.2%`, within noise).
- [`normal-fragment-map.glsl`](../src/shaders/normal-fragment-map.glsl) samples the layer's normal map through `sampleTerrainNormal()` from [`terrain-normal-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/terrain-normal-pars.glsl). The UV is world `(x, -z)`, rotated by minus the layer rotation and divided by its tile size, so tiles continue across chunks and LODs. After strength is applied, the sampled XY is rotated back by the same angle, so the relief stays lit correctly in the unrotated tangent frame. The V axis runs along `-Z` like the chunk UV, so Three's tangent frame, built from `vNormalMapUv`, still matches. Only the selected layer is fetched. The maps are KTX2 with the normal's X in the color channels and Y in alpha ([Assets](ASSETS.md#encoding-pipeline)), so the shader reads `(r, a)` and rebuilds `z = sqrt(1 - x² - y²)` before applying the strength. `textureGrad()` takes gradients computed outside the branch, because GLSL ES 3.00 only indexes sampler arrays with constants and derivatives are undefined in non-uniform branches. Strength is then faded with distance from `uCamera` between `uTerrainNormalFade.x` and `.y`: by default full within `150` units and gone beyond `500` (`TERRAIN_NORMAL_FADE`). A fade that reaches too far can let the fine fabric patterns alias into moiré at mid distance; an early `400`-unit fade with the original repeat did. On the sea layer the UV first snakes across the ribs, and the waves' normal is added after the fade ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)).
- [`curved-light-terminator.glsl`](../src/shaders/curved-light-terminator.glsl) is inserted into `lights_fragment_begin` (see below).

### Sea Ice

The sea of the ice biome freezes around its lands, and its edge breaks into floes. [`sea-ice-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-ice-pars-fragment.glsl) draws it from `color-fragment.glsl` (`applySeaIce()`), for sea pixels where `vIceValue` is above `0`; [`sea-ice-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-ice-pars.glsl), shared with the vertex shader, holds the uniforms and the shelf. [`src/seaIcePolicy.js`](../src/seaIcePolicy.js) mirrors the sheet for the debug map and the tests.

- **Shelf:** the sheet covers the sea where the raw height plus the shelf is above `0`. The shelf grows from `0` at the ice border to `shelf` (`15.3` units of depth) once the ice field reaches `fade` (`0.19`), so the sheet narrows to the coast where the ice ends. A wobble of `edgeNoise` units of depth (`1.1`, one field of the [noise tile](#noise-tile) at `edgeFrequency` `0.145` per unit) frays its edge.
- **Night freeze:** the sea freezes further at night. `getSeaIceNightGrowth(timeOfDay)` is `0` from noon to sunset, rises smoothly through the night to `1` at sunrise, and falls back to `0` by noon (`SEA_ICE_NIGHT`: `0.75`, `0.25`, `0.5`, on a flat horizon; midnight is halfway). `getSeaIceShelfAt()` deepens the shelf by `nightShelf` (`22` units of depth) times that growth, which more than quadruples the sheet's area at sunrise (measured over four seeds: from about `18%` to `76%` of the ice biome's sea); the floe band moves seaward with the edge, so plates freeze in ahead of it and come loose again behind it. The floes freeze harder too: `getSeaIceBandAt()` deepens the band by `nightBand` (`9`, twice the daytime band at sunrise), so the plates reach further seaward and pack closer, and `getSeaIceCrackScaleAt()` narrows both crack widths by the share `nightCrack` (`0.6`, to `40%` at sunrise). Estimated from the plates' mean coverage and the crack widths over four seeds' ice sea, the ice covers about `25%` of the ice biome's sea at noon and `90%` at sunrise (`83%` with the shelf alone). It follows the time of day, not the apparent sunrise, so the camera height never moves the edge. `World.tic()` writes the shelf and band into `uSeaIceShape.x` and `.z`, and the cracks into `uSeaIceEdge.x` and `.y`, every frame after `DayNight` (`updateSeaIceNight()`), and the vertex shader's still sea follows them.
- **Floes:** beyond the edge, a band `band` (`9`) units of depth deep by day, tapered like the shelf, breaks into plates: a Voronoi of jittered points in cells `cellSize` (`15`) units wide (Inigo Quilez's two-pass distance to the nearest cell edge, with a `pcg3d` hash of the integer cell). Each cell is ice where the relative depth into the band is below the cell's random value, so plates come loose seaward, and water cracks open along the cell edges, from `crackMin` (`0.25` units) at the sheet to `crackMax` (`4.3`) at the band's outer limit by day, fading into the sheet over half a band. A box filter of the pixel's ground footprint antialiases the cracks; where a cell spans less than about two pixels, the plates blend into their mean coverage instead of sparkling.
- **No dark line:** the ice mixes directly with the sea color at its depth, and the water between the plates takes an icy tint (`water`) that fades seaward and toward the ice border. The sheet, floe, and water colors (`#aed3e5`, `#ffffff`, `#2fe1ee`) are `uSeaIceColors`; the sheet's turns into the floes' across its edge.
- **Still sea:** the coverage removes the foam lines and switches the normal layer to the snow's; the waves stop under the sheet and the band, with a few units of depth of margin for coarse LODs (`getSeaIceStill()`), in the vertex shader and, with the coverage, in the fragment ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)).
- **Settings:** `params.seaIce` (`createSeaIceSettings()`), written by `updateSeaIceUniforms()` into `uSeaIceShape` (the shelf and band at the current time of day, fade, cell size), `uSeaIceEdge` (the cracks at the current time of day, wobble), and `uSeaIceColors`. The **Biomes > Ice > Frozen sea** GUI folder edits them live: **Sheet depth** is the daytime shelf and **Night growth** the extra depth at sunrise (`0` keeps the sheet still), **Night floe band** the band's extra depth at sunrise, and **Night crack closing** the share of the cracks that closes at sunrise (`0` for both keeps the floes as by day). The debug mode pauses the cycle at `0.3`, where the sheet is about `90%` grown. Placement keeps boats out of the ice biome ([Terrain](TERRAIN.md#placement)); sea rocks stand in it. Where the [deep ocean](TERRAIN.md#deep-ocean) borders the ice, its slope deepens the ice's sea, so the sheet narrows to the shore there.
- **Cost:** sea pixels outside the floe band evaluate nothing; band pixels add one [noise tile](#noise-tile) sample (the wobble) and 18 hashes.

### Sea Surface

The sea moves: Gerstner waves lift its vertices near the airplane and tilt its normals everywhere, its ribbed normal map snakes, foam lines ride the crests, and the coast's foam lines follow its depth. The sea and the [deep ocean](TERRAIN.md#deep-ocean) have their own settings, rougher and calmer regions drift across both, the waves calm toward every coast, and the frozen sea stays still. [`sea-surface-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-surface-pars.glsl), guarded and shared by the terrain's two stages and the boats, holds the waves; [`sea-ripple-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-ripple-pars-fragment.glsl) the fragment details. The pure rules and defaults are in [`src/seaSurfacePolicy.js`](../src/seaSurfacePolicy.js) (`createSeaSurfaceSettings()`, `params.seaSurface`), and `updateSeaSurfaceUniforms()` ([`src/sharedUniforms.js`](../src/sharedUniforms.js)) writes them into the `uSea*` uniforms when the shared uniforms are built and from the GUI.

- **Sea types:** every setting comes twice, index `0` for the sea and `1` for the deep ocean. `getSeaOceanMask()` blends them over `oceanBlend` (`0.05`) of the deep ocean field (`vOceanValue`) inside its border, so the boats, which keep out of the deep ocean, only ever float on the sea. A pixel evaluates one type, or both across the blend.
- **Waves:** four Gerstner waves per type, which `getSeaWaveComponents()` derives from a few settings: the dominant `wavelength` and its ratios `1`, `0.62`, `0.38`, `0.24` (`SEA_WAVE_RATIOS`); the wind `direction` plus `0`, `0.45`, `-0.75`, and `1` times the `spread` (`SEA_WAVE_ANGLES`); amplitudes in the same ratios, summing to `amplitude` (the highest crest); the deep-water dispersion `ω = √(g k) × speed` (`g = 9.81`), so longer waves travel faster; and a horizontal reach of `steepness / (4 k)`, so the crests squeeze the surface by at most the steepness and never fold over. Defaults: the sea `3.05` units high, `68` long, steepness `0.55`, spread `40°`, speed `0.6`; the deep ocean `6`, `72`, `0.7`, `30°`; both from `35°`. `uSeaWaves` holds each component's direction, wavenumber, and angular speed, `uSeaWaveSizes` its amplitude and reach.
- **Regions:** `getSeaState()` reads two `snoise` octaves at the seeded world position (`uBiomeOffset.xy` plus a constant offset), `regions.scale` (`2500`) units wide, drifting at `regions.drift` (`4` units per second) toward `regions.direction`, sharpened by `contrast` (`1.6`) into a sea state from `0` (calm) to `1` (rough). Its layers are broad, so the vertex shader evaluates it into the varying `vSeaState`. Each type scales its waves from `calm` to `rough` times their amplitude (`0.35`–`1.3` for the sea, `0.3`–`1.6` for the deep ocean) and reaches its steepness only in the roughest regions (`uSeaWaveShape`).
- **Coast and ice:** the waves' `amount` grows from `0` at `coast.start` to `1` at `coast.full` units of depth (`1.5` to `10` for the sea, `4.9` to `16` for the deep ocean, whose islets calm them too), times one minus the [frozen sea](#sea-ice)'s stillness: `getSeaIceStill()` in the vertex shader and, in the fragment, where `vIceValue` is above `0`, the larger of it and the ice coverage.
- **Vertices:** [`project-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/project-vertex.glsl) adds the Gerstner displacement to the sea's vertices (raw height below `0`), fully within `vertex.fadeStart` (`183`) and not at all beyond `vertex.fadeEnd` (`240`) units of the airplane, horizontally. That stays inside the LOD `0` block, at least `256` units around the airplane ([Terrain](TERRAIN.md#level-of-detail)), so the moved vertices never meet T-junctions; the GUI stops at `240`. `wPosition` and `vShadowPosition` stay flat. `ChunkManager` pads the curved culling spheres by `getSeaWaveBound()` every frame.
- **Normals:** `color-fragment.glsl` evaluates the waves again per pixel (`getSeaWaves()`), where each component fades out as the pixel's ground size times its wavenumber goes from `1` to `2.5`, before it aliases. `getSeaWaveNormal()` is the Gerstner normal; [`normal-fragment-map.glsl`](../src/shaders/normal-fragment-map.glsl) turns it into the tangent frame (T along `+X`, B along `-Z`) and adds its slopes to the sea map's after the map's distance fade, so the swell reads up to the horizon. Only the sea layer gets it.
- **Ripple drift:** before sampling the sea layer, its world UV slides by `uSeaRippleDrift × uTime` along the texture's V axis on the ground (a quarter turn from `uTerrainNormalRotation[0]`), so the ribs move over time even where the waves calm. `params.seaSurface.rippleDrift` (`-2.5` units per second by default; the sign picks the direction) is one speed for both sea types: a speed that differed across the deep ocean's border would stretch the texture there more and more as time grows. It costs one multiply-add per sea pixel.
- **Ripples:** then `getSeaRippleWarp()` moves the world UV across the ribs (the texture's U axis on the ground, `uTerrainNormalRotation[0]`) by `amplitude × amount × boost × sin(2π / wavelength × (along − speed × t) + irregularity × noise)`: the ribs snake, still and straight where the waves calm. The noise is `vSeaRippleNoise` (`getSeaRippleNoise()`, one `snoise` per sea vertex at `irregularityScale` per unit, about `50` units wide). `textureGrad()` keeps the unwarped gradients, and the warp only runs within the normal maps' fade end. The sea state scales the map's strength by up to `± stateStrength` (`boost`), never below `minimum` (`uSeaMinimum.y`), so calm water keeps its ripples. Defaults: the sea `0.5` units across, `12` along, `1.5` units per second, irregularity `2`, `0.02`, `± 0.35`, at least `0.8`; the deep ocean `0.7`, `18`, `2`, `2.5`, `0.015`, `± 0.45`, at least `1`.
- **Crest lines:** `getSeaCrestLine()` draws foam lines along the crests of the waves, toward `foamColor`: no noise, so they move with the crests. They follow the ridges of the `waves` longest waves (`uSeaCrestWaves`, accumulated in `SeaWaves.ridge` and `curvature`): with all four they break into short strokes on every crest, the short waves' too; with the longest two they run long along the swell; the default three sits between. Across the ridge is the direction where the surface bends down the most, the eigenvector of the height's most negative curvature, which stays well defined on the ridge itself, where the gradient vanishes and points anywhere (an across direction taken from the gradient left a gap along the middle of every line, which then looked like two close lines). A pixel's distance from the ridge is the gradient along that direction over that curvature, exact on a parabola, so each line is one stroke centered on the crest; the line is `width` units wide on each side, box-filtered by the pixel's ground size, so lines thinner than a pixel fade instead of flickering. It shows where the height of those waves rises above `threshold` (over `softness`) of the highest they reach there, so a line lengthens as crests meet and shortens as they part; it narrows only near its ends (its half-width times the square root of the crest presence), so the half-width sets its thickness without changing its length. In the calmest regions only `minimum` of that presence stays (`uSeaMinimum.x`): the threshold rises toward the very top, so calm water keeps a few short lines. The coast and the ice calm them with the waves' amount. Two noises, drawn in a frame that rides the longest wave (the rest position minus its travel at its phase speed), so they move with the crests, animate and fray the lines; they run only within the line's reach. The flicker, two octaves from two fields of the [noise tile](#noise-tile) at `flickerScale` per unit (and `2.1` times it) drifting apart at `flickerSpeed` so it changes shape, hides about `gaps` of the lines, which narrow where it closes, so strokes appear, grow, shrink, and vanish. The fray, two fields of one finer noise tile sample at `frayScale` per unit, varies the width and nudges the line sideways by up to `fray` of the half-width, so its edges are ragged; it fades out as the pixel's ground size times `frayScale` goes from `0.15` to `0.35`. Defaults: the sea `0.64`, `0.5`, `0.6` units, intensity `0.85`, minimum `0.55`, flicker `0.35` at `0.035` and `0.25`, fray `0.5` at `0.5`; the deep ocean `0.54`, `0.442`, `0.91`, `0.9`, `0.6`, flicker `0.3` at `0.025` and `0.25`, fray `0.5` at `0.35`; both on all four waves (`uSeaCrestLines`, `uSeaCrestFlicker`, `uSeaCrestFray`).
- **Breaking foam:** the other crest foam, `getSeaBreakingFoam()`. The crests break where they squeeze the surface (`SeaWaves.squeeze`) beyond `threshold ± softness` of the largest squeeze the waves reach there (`SeaWaves.peak`), with the same calm-water `minimum` presence as the lines (`uSeaMinimum.z`). The foam stays where it formed while the crest moves on, and dissolves over `trail` seconds: it is the strongest breaking now and at four lags of the recent past (`getSeaBreakingLags()`, evenly spaced, the last a fifth of the trail before its end), weighted `0.8`, `0.6`, `0.4`, and `0.2` by their age, so it trails behind the crests. The past squeezes cost no trigonometry: `uSeaWaveLagCos` and `uSeaWaveLagSin` hold each component's `cos ωΔ` and `sin ωΔ` for the four lags, so `sin(θ + ωΔ) = sin θ cos ωΔ + cos θ sin ωΔ` reuses the current sine and cosine (`SeaWaves.squeezeLag`). Fresh foam is solid; as it ages, a lace of two `snoise` octaves at `scale` per unit, drifting apart so it changes shape, opens holes in it until it is gone. The lace is drawn on the rest position, so it rides the water's orbits near the airplane; where it would alias (the pixel's ground size times `scale` from `0.15` to `0.35`) the foam turns into its mean coverage. The coast and the ice calm it with the waves' amount. Defaults: the sea `0.62`, `0.12`, intensity `0.9`, lace `0.15`, trail `2.5` s, minimum `0.35`; the deep ocean `0.6`, `0.12`, `0.95`, `0.1`, `1.5` s, `0.45` (`uSeaBreaking`).
- **Crest foam:** `crestFoam` (`SEA_CREST_FOAM_STYLES`, `uSeaCrestFoam`) picks the lines (the default), the breaking foam, or both, which then take the larger coverage.
- **Foam lines:** `getSeaRipple()` (see [Terrain](#terrain)) reads its band, motion, sharpness, intensity, wobble, and dashes per type, and the sea state scales them by up to `± stateBoost` (`0.3`); the dashes come from one field of the [noise tile](#noise-tile) (the former hard-coded sine ran along `x` only). The deep ocean's defaults reproduce the former hard-coded lines. The sea's draw fewer, sharper, brighter lines farther out: a band rising from `10.1` to `10.9` units deep and fading from `7.8` to `17.4`, `4.2` lines per unit of depth, sharpness `13.9`, intensity `1.8`, a wobble of `2.65` radians at `0.25` per unit, and dashes `2` strong at `0.071` per unit.
- **Boats:** with `SCENERY_SEA_WAVES` (the scenery catalog's define; the clouds never compile it), `getSceneryFloat()` in [`scenery-instance-pars-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-instance-pars-vertex.glsl) floats the instances of `SCENERY_BOAT_TYPE`. A boat reads the sea's depth at its center from the blue byte of its tint (`getSceneryBoatDepth()`; placement packs it with `packBoatTint()`, [Terrain](TERRAIN.md#placement)), and `getSeaFloatPose()` evaluates the sea's waves at its flat base with the same sea state and vertex fade as the terrain: the base moves with the water point under its center, and the near meshes tilt `+Y` onto the water's normal. Impostors only bob, since their baked frames cannot tilt; beyond the vertex fade nothing moves, so the cross-fade between the two stays seamless. Their shadow casters stay still, like the flat receivers of the sea.
- **Debug view:** `debugView` (`SEA_SURFACE_DEBUG_VIEWS`) paints the sea's pixels with the sea state, the deep ocean mask (blue to red), the waves' amount, or the crest squeeze (times `2`), which the crest lines no longer read.
- **GUI:** the top-level **Sea surface** folder edits everything live: debug view, foam color, **Crest foam** (lines, breaking, or both), deep ocean blend, **Ripple drift (units/s)**, **Regions (sea state)**, **Moving vertices**, then **Sea** and **Deep ocean**, each with **Waves**, **Toward the coast**, **Ripples (normal map)**, **Crest lines**, **Breaking foam**, and **Foam lines**. **Foam around rocks** holds the [sea foam](#sea-foam) controls. **Copy sea JSON** logs and copies the settings, **Reset sea** restores the defaults.
- **Cost:** the vertex work (three `snoise` per terrain vertex, the waves on the sea's vertices near the airplane) and the boats measured nothing. The per-pixel waves of the sea's fragments cost about `12%` of the frame over open sea: measured with `pnpm bench` (seed `s762`, `time=0.35`), `39.2` against `34.4` ms mean at `3840 × 2160` and `12.5` against `11.2` at `1920 × 1080` (`--dpr 1`). The new fragment code alone, present but never run, cost about `3%`; the ripple warp measured nothing.
- **Known limits:** Boat shadows do not bob. The wave crests do not turn toward the coast; the foam lines, drawn on the depth contours, do. The sea's only sun glint is Three.js's GGX specular at the terrain's roughness of `1`: a broad glare toward a low sun, which the shadows and the [low-sun occlusion](#low-sun-terrain-occlusion) remove where the sun is hidden. Across the deep ocean blend the two types' settings mix, so their wave phases interfere there.

### Curved-World Lighting

The curvature in `project-vertex.glsl` moves vertices down by `R * (1 - cos(dist / R))` but used to leave normals flat, so distant terrain was lit as if the world were flat. Terrain now shades as the sphere it is drawn on:

- **Bent normal (vertex):** after the curvature distance is known, `objectNormal` is rotated about `cross(up, awayDirection)` by `dist / uCurvature`, the same angle the surface tilts away from `uCamera`. The result overwrites `vNormal`. Chunk meshes are only translated, so object-space normals are world-space and `normalMatrix` applies. The derivative-based normal-map TBN uses the already curved positions and stays consistent. The unperturbed sphere normal is passed in the terrain-only varying `vSphereNormal`, which is declared in the injected code rather than in `common.glsl`.
- **Terminator (fragment):** [`src/curvedLights.js`](../src/curvedLights.js) builds a copy of Three.js's `lights_fragment_begin` with the terminator appended after the exact r186 line `getDirectionalLightInfo( directionalLight, directLight );`. For every directional light, `directLight.color` is scaled by `smoothstep(-0.02, 0.06, dot(directLight.direction, vSphereNormal))`. The sun or moon therefore contributes nothing where it is below that fragment's local horizon, even on slopes facing it. At sunset, distant terrain toward the sun stays lit while nearby terrain is already dark. `curvedLights.js` warns in the console if a Three.js upgrade removes the hook line. Every terrain and scenery material gets its lighting from `createSceneryLighting({ shadows, sunOcclusion, ambientScale, translucent })`, which returns the material defines, the fragment declarations, the `lights_fragment_begin` replacement, and the extra uniforms; each material provides its own view-space `vSphereNormal`. With `shadows` (`{ taps, position, selfBias }`: the PCF tap counts and GLSL expressions for the flat shadow position and self bias; terrain, scenery impostors, near scenery meshes), it also applies the [scenery](#scenery-shadows) and [cloud](#cloud-shadows) shadows after the terminator. Without them (`SCENERY_NO_SHADOWS`; cloud impostors and meshes), `ambientScale` can multiply the ambient irradiance (the r186 line `vec3 irradiance = getAmbientLightIrradiance( ambientLightColor );`, also checked with a console warning) by the `uSceneryAmbientScale` uniform; the `SCENERY_AMBIENT_SCALE` define keeps materials with and without it in separate programs while their GLSL text stays the same.
- **Glare in shadow:** the glare is Three.js's direct GGX specular, and only it reads `material.multiScatteringCompensation`, set just before the r186 line `IncidentLight directLight;` (also checked with a console warning). The shadowed lighting saves it there (`sceneryMultiScattering`) and, in the loop, rescales it for the shadowing light by `sceneryShadowSpecular`: the scenery and cloud lookups with their specular share in place of their strength, `1 − (1 − lookup) × uSceneryShadowSpecular` times the same with `uCloudShadowSpecular` (see [Scenery Shadows](#scenery-shadows)). `directLight.color` already carries the capped shadow, so a full shadow keeps `1 − strength` of the diffuse light but none of the glare; across the penumbra the two multiply. The diffuse term, including its `(1 − F)`, is untouched. With `SUN_OCCLUSION_STEPS` the sun's light and glare are scaled once more by the [low-sun occlusion](#low-sun-terrain-occlusion).
- **Loop rule:** Three.js unrolls the directional light loop by copying its body without its braces (`unrollLoops()` in `WebGLProgram.js`), and its pattern ends the body at the first closing brace. Code added to the loop (`addToDirectionalLights()`) may therefore only assign, call, and use `#ifdef`: a declaration would repeat for every light (sun and moon), a brace would cut the loop short. Values per fragment are computed before the loop; [`test/curvedLights.test.js`](../test/curvedLights.test.js) checks the added code.
- **Dark lights:** both lights are in every lit program, but by day the moon's uniform color is exactly `0` (its elevation fade, [Day/Night Cycle](#daynight-cycle)), and by night the sun's. Every directional light's contribution, the cloud translucency included, scales with its color, so the light loop prefixes the directional block's `RE_Direct()` (the r186 call, checked with a console warning) with `if ( any( notEqual( directionalLight.color, vec3( 0.0 ) ) ) )`: a branch on a uniform, the same on every pixel, that skips the dark light's GGX lighting with the same result. Only the twilight, when both shine, runs both. Hiding the moon outright saved about `7%` of the frame at `2400 × 1100`, but would change the light count and recompile every lit program at each hand-over. Materials outside `createSceneryLighting()`, such as the airplane, keep three's loop.
- **Variants:** the terrain adds `sunOcclusion: { steps }` (`SUN_OCCLUSION_STEPS`), the clouds `translucent` (`CLOUD_TRANSLUCENCY`, see [Clouds](#clouds)). Each path's GLSL text always holds both blocks, so variants differ only by their defines. `uSunDirection`, the shared world direction toward the sun that `DayNight` writes, is declared in the lighting's fragment declarations; both features tell the sun from the moon by it.
- Ambient light is unaffected except on clouds, whose `uSceneryAmbientScale` (`params.clouds.ambient`) lifts it. Scenery and cloud impostors bend their baked normals, near meshes bend their vertex normals, and all use the terminator like terrain. The airplane and trails still use flat lighting; the boats, as scenery, use the curved terminator.

### Impostor Scenery

Trees, palms, cacti, rocks, ice spikes, and boats are octahedral impostors: each instance is one camera-facing quad (2 triangles), shaded from baked color and normal images. Clouds use the same pipeline (see [Clouds](#clouds)).

- **Catalogs:** [`src/impostors/impostorCatalogs.js`](../src/impostors/impostorCatalogs.js) describes each family drawn through the pipeline: `SCENERY_IMPOSTORS` and `CLOUD_IMPOSTORS`. A catalog holds the type count, the atlas columns and rows, the hemisphere the family is seen from (`1` upper, `-1` lower), `createSources(type, lodCount)`, and the shader defines that adapt the shared shaders to the family. `getCatalogSources(catalog, type)` builds each type's sources once, every near-mesh level at once, and caches them for the page: the atlas bakes and the near meshes share the same geometries, so they are built and uploaded once (about `110` ms less main-thread work at startup in Node on an Apple M1). Consumers never dispose or modify them. `setCatalogSources(catalog, type, levels)` gives a type sources from a loaded model instead (the boat); it throws if the type's sources were already built, so it must run before the bake. `getCatalogDefines()` turns the layout into `IMPOSTOR_TYPE_COUNT`, `IMPOSTOR_ATLAS_COLUMNS`, and `IMPOSTOR_ATLAS_ROWS`. Every material built from a catalog carries its defines. Materials of different families share their `onBeforeCompile` source, so the defines also keep their compiled programs apart. The unshadowed variant adds `SCENERY_NO_SHADOWS` for the same reason. Both catalogs add `SCENERY_PALETTE` and `SCENERY_PALETTE_SIZE` (see [Scenery Palettes](#scenery-palettes)); the cloud catalog also adds `SCENERY_FACE_AIRPLANE` and `SCENERY_NO_ATMOSPHERE_CLAMP`.

- **Sources:** [`src/impostors/impostorArchetypes.js`](../src/impostors/impostorArchetypes.js) builds ten types from Three.js primitives, and a stand-in boat (an oval hull and a cabin) that is baked only when the boat model fails to load and lets the Node tests build every type. They are merged, smooth-shaded, lightly noise-deformed, and carry vertex colors with a baked vertical occlusion. Colors come from `COLORS` in that file; all scenery has a wooden-toy palette. The sea rock is a group of three stones, tapered cones wide at the base with a narrower, blunt top (`42%` of the base radius), leaning slightly. The ice spikes are stalagmites of ice grouped by two (`ICE_SPIKES_TWO`) or three (`ICE_SPIKES_THREE`): tapered cylinders almost closed at the tip (`6%` of the base radius, because a closed tip pinches the normals), the tallest `4.5` units high, the others shorter and leaning away from it. They stay stout, because distant mips and the shadow casters lose thin parts. The palm (`PALM`, for the deep ocean's islets) is a toy palm: a trunk of six truncated cones, each wider at its top so the rings read as carved wood, rising upright and bowing toward local `+Z` with the crown almost back over the base (the recentering would otherwise move the base off the instance position); seven chunky fronds, closed boxes tapered to a point, folded in a V along the midrib and arched over; a small crown and three coconuts. Every type but the boat (`SCENERY_PAINTED_TYPES`) is baked in a neutral linear gray, `SCENERY_PAINT_BASE` (`0.5`) times the occlusion; their colors come from the [scenery palettes](#scenery-palettes) at runtime. The sea rock's smaller stone is baked at `0.64` of that gray and every other band of the layered rock at `0.7` (the ratio of its former wood tones, `#cfa878` under `#e3c9a0`), as are the ice spikes' shorter spikes, so they keep two tones under any palette color. Every part also carries a `paint` attribute, the paint mask: `1` on the round tree's crown and blobs, the conifer's cones, the palm's crown and fronds, and every part of the cacti, the rocks, and the ice spikes, `0` on tree and palm trunks (and the palm's coconuts, baked at `0.64` of the gray) (every part has it, because `mergeGeometries()` needs matching attributes). Bounding spheres are recentered on the Y axis so yaw rotates around the base. The same geometry is baked and drawn as LOD 0 by the [near scenery meshes](#near-scenery-meshes), so segment counts stay low: 740 triangles for the round tree, 292 for the conifer, 636 and 876 for the one- and two-arm cacti, 320 for the boulder, 264 for the layered rock, 360 for the sea rock, 140 and 210 for the two- and three-spike ice spikes, and 988 for the palm. The cactus trunk keeps 18 radial segments, two per rib, so its 9 ribs do not alias.
- **Boat sources:** the boat comes from its model, `public/boat-toy/boat.glb` ([Assets](ASSETS.md#boat)). `World.init()` passes it to `createBoatSources()` ([`src/impostors/boatSources.js`](../src/impostors/boatSources.js)) and the result to `setCatalogSources()`. Each level (the meshes `boat-lod0` and `boat-lod1`, `BOAT_LEVEL_NAMES`) becomes an indexed geometry with float `position`, `normal`, and `uv` (quantized values decoded, the node transform applied), a white `color`, and a zero `paint`, scaled to `SCENERY_CONFIG.boat.length` (`12` units) along its bow axis (+Z), with the keel on y `0` and the sphere centered on the Y axis like the built sources. `geometry.userData.map` keeps the model's base color (sRGB): the bake and the near meshes sample it at the uv. LOD 0 has 16,000 triangles and LOD 1 3,000. The boat is not painted and keeps the model's colors: its wood detail strength and brightness variation are `0`, and placement gives it a white tint.
- **Types:** the type indices, atlas layout, and instance stride live in [`src/impostors/impostorTypes.js`](../src/impostors/impostorTypes.js).
- **Bake:** [`src/impostors/impostorBaker.js`](../src/impostors/impostorBaker.js) bakes one catalog (`bakeImpostorAtlas(renderer, { catalog, frames, frameSize, detail })`, scenery by default). `SceneryImpostors` runs it in `World.init()`, before chunks exist, and again when a **Wood detail** control is released. `setImpostorAtlas()` swaps the new atlas into the shared material's uniforms and disposes the old one without recompiling; the new atlas must keep the catalog and frame count.
  - It renders every type from the grid directions of a view layout ([`src/impostors/octahedral.js`](../src/impostors/octahedral.js)) with an orthographic camera framed on the bounding sphere plus a `4%` margin. A layout has `framesX × framesY` frames per type:
    - **hemi-octahedral** (`createHemiOctViews(frames, hemisphere)`, the default with the catalog's hemisphere): a whole hemisphere on a square grid. The count must be even, so no frame looks at the degenerate pole.
    - **frontal** (`createFrontalViews()`): only the band within `±azimuth` of local `-Z` and from the horizon down to `elevation`, for clouds (see [Clouds](#clouds)).
    - Frames sit on grid corners, so the edge frames bake the layout's limits, and directions outside the layout clamp to its edge.
  - `getMaxImpostorFrameSize()` shrinks `frameSize` when the supersampled bake target or the atlas would exceed `renderer.capabilities.maxTextureSize`. The atlas records its catalog, view layout, and final frame size. `getAtlasLayout()` gives the block and atlas sizes.
  - `SceneryImpostors` passes `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES_DESKTOP` or `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES_MOBILE`, both `12` (144 views per type): desktop impostors appear only beyond the near meshes (`300` units). The shadow casters read the same atlas. The material turns the atlas layout into defines with `getViewDefines()` (`IMPOSTOR_FRAMES_X`, `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES_Y`, and `IMPOSTOR_LOWER_HEMISPHERE` or `IMPOSTOR_FRONTAL_VIEWS` with its two angles), so the bake and the shader always agree. A re-bake must keep the catalog and layout (`isSameViews()`).
  - **Wood detail:** with a `detail` texture (`USE_DETAIL`), the bake fragment shader ([`impostor-bake-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/impostor-bake-fragment.glsl)) samples the color of [`white_oak_veneer_diff_1k.ktx2`](../src/textures/white_oak/white_oak_veneer_diff_1k.ktx2) triplanarly in object space. The function lives in [`scenery-detail-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-detail-pars-fragment.glsl), which `impostorBaker.js` substitutes for the `#include <scenery_detail_pars_fragment>` placeholder, and which the near meshes also use. The texture is loaded as sRGB, so samples are linear.
    - The texture grain runs along V. The side projections (`yz`, `yx`) map U to height, so the grain runs horizontally around trunks and across the clouds' flat faces.
    - Settings are per type: `applyDetail(color, position, normal, settings)` takes `settings = (repeats per unit, strength, normalized)`. The bake sets `uDetailSettings` before each type's frames from `detail.types[type]` (`{ scale, color, normalized }`); the near meshes read `uSceneryDetail[type]` in the vertex shader and pass it on in `vSceneryDetail`.
    - It multiplies the part's vertex color by `mix(1, texture, color)`, as Three.js combines `map` and `vertexColors`, so the parts keep their relative palette and darken by the texture's color. The normal is not perturbed.
    - With `normalized` (above `0.5`), the sample is first divided by the texture's mean color, read from its smallest mip with `textureLod()`. The grain then varies around `1`, so it adds lines without darkening or tinting the base color: the light cloud color, or the trees' neutral bake that the palette colors multiply.
    - `params.impostorDetail` is keyed by type (`SCENERY_TYPE_KEYS`). The trees and the palm use the clouds' look, `scale 0.15` (about one repeat per tree, as `0.012` is per cloud), `color 0.6`, normalized. Cacti and rocks keep `scale 0.01`, `color 0.1`, not normalized. Releasing a control in a type's **Scenery > Wood detail** folder re-bakes the atlas and rewrites the near meshes' `uSceneryDetail` in place (`SceneryImpostors.applyDetail()`).
    - The normalized grain can exceed `1`; the trees' gray base of `0.5` keeps the baked result below white, so the 8-bit atlas never clips where the near mesh (float) does not.
    - The boat's `color` is `0`: no grain on the model's colors.
    - The detail is baked, so it costs nothing per frame and rotates and scales with each instance. Its finest visible scale is limited by the `64` px frames: impostors keep only the broad grain bands, the near meshes show it all.
  - **Color map:** a source with `userData.map` is drawn with a second bake material that shares the first one's uniforms and adds `USE_SOURCE_MAP`: the vertex shader passes `uv` as `vSourceUv`, and the fragment multiplies the vertex color by `uSourceMap` before the wood detail. The second material is created only when a source has a map, and both are disposed after the bake.
  - It renders one type at a time into a `2×` supersampled MRT target (`count: 3`) the size of one type block: at most `2048` px, which stays inside mobile texture limits. The target holds sRGB-encoded albedo with coverage in alpha, object-space normal with normalized depth in alpha, and the paint mask in a single-channel (`RedFormat`, R8) third attachment: the sources' `paint` with `SCENERY_PALETTE`, `0` otherwise (clouds, cloud shadows). Three.js sets every attachment up from its own texture's format. The depth is the signed distance from the image plane toward the viewer, divided by the frame radius and stored as `d * 0.5 + 0.5`; the impostor fragment uses it to rebuild the surface position for the shadow lookup.
  - A resolve pass writes that type's block of the atlas (`uBlockOrigin`). Its material, quad, and compiled program are kept across bakes. Three.js regenerates a target's mipmaps after every render into it, so only the last block's render generates them, once for the whole atlas; the mip storage is allocated on the first `setRenderTarget()`, before generation is switched off. It box-downsamples to fractional coverage, averages the normal and depth weighted by coverage, and dilates the color and normal of the nearest covered texel into empty texels of the same frame, so bilinear filtering and mipmaps do not pull in dark halos. The paint mask is stored premultiplied by coverage (empty texels `0`), so filtering and every mip level average it correctly against the coverage the impostor divides by: no trunk-colored rim around tree crowns and no gray rim around cacti at any distance.
  - The result is two mipmapped RGBA8 atlases and one R8 paint-mask atlas (`atlas.paint`), laid out as `4 × 3` type blocks of `64` px frames (eleven types, so one block is free for one more type before another row or column): `3072 × 2304` on desktop and mobile, about `85` MB with mips. The palm moved the atlas from `5 × 2` blocks (`3840 × 1536`, about `71` MB), which ten types filled; the two ice spike types had added the fifth column, as the sea rock had added the fourth. Under a `4096` texture limit `64` px frames still fit (up to `85`); under a `2048` one, `getMaxImpostorFrameSize()` shrinks them to `42` px. `setImpostorAtlas()` also swaps `uImpostorPaint`.
- **Vertex (`impostor-vertex.glsl`, replacing `project_vertex`):**
  1. Reads per-instance `aInstanceA` (chunk-local base, scale) and `aInstanceB` (yaw, type, packed tint, stretch). The attributes, `getSceneryTint()`, and `getSceneryMeshFade()` live in [`scenery-instance-pars-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-instance-pars-vertex.glsl), shared with the near meshes; it includes `getCurvatureBend()` ([`curvature-bend.glsl`](../src/shaders/curvature-bend.glsl)).
  2. Moves a boat's base with the sea waves (`getSceneryFloat()`, [Sea Surface](#sea-surface)), applies the terrain curvature at the base, and places the bounding-sphere center along the bent sphere normal.
  3. Builds the quad facing the eye: perpendicular to the ray from `cameraPosition` to the center, with its right axis from the camera's up axis. A quad parallel to the image plane would cut instances away from the screen center, where the bounding sphere projects up to `1 / cos(angle off axis)` larger onto it (about `1.5×` in the screen corners). The half-size is the radius of the sphere's tangent cone in that plane, `R × d / √(d² − R²)` (at most `4R`), so the whole silhouette fits at any distance and screen position.
  4. Converts the camera and each vertex into the baked local frame by undoing the bend, yaw, scale, and stretch.
  5. Picks the three frames around the view direction with barycentric weights (`selectImpostorFrames()`, the same triangle blend as `getFrameBlend()`). Each frame UV comes from intersecting the view ray with that frame's image plane.
  6. Scales instances to zero between `uImpostorFarFade.x` and `.y` units from `uCamera`, well before the fog end: `800` to `950` for scenery (`SCENERY_IMPOSTOR_FAR_FADE` in `impostorMaterial.js`), and inside the field radius for clouds. Near the eye it computes the mesh fade (see [Near Scenery Meshes](#near-scenery-meshes)) and collapses the quad where the mesh has fully taken over.
  7. Multiplies the tint by a position-based brightness variation: two octaves of `snoise` at the flat world base, scaled by `uImpostorVariationFrequency` (default `0.01`). The noise is stretched by `1.6` and clamped to `±1`. The tint is multiplied by `exp2(noise × uImpostorVariationAmount[type])`, so the amount is in stops, one float per type. The default `0.8` (rocks) spans about `×0.57` to `×1.74`; the trees, palms, and cacti default to `0.35`, so their palette colors stay recognizable, and the boat to `0`, so it keeps the model's colors. The GUI allows up to `2`. Neighbouring instances share a shade while distant groups differ, on top of the per-instance random tint from placement. The uniforms belong to `SceneryImpostors` (from `params.impostorVariation`) and update live, with no placement job.
  8. With `SCENERY_PALETTE`, `getSceneryPaletteTints()` splits that tint into the trunk tint (`vTint`) and the painted tint (`vPaintTint`); see [Scenery Palettes](#scenery-palettes). Unpainted types get the plain tint in both.
- **Fragment:**
  - `impostor-color-fragment.glsl` first discards the pixels that the near mesh keeps, before any atlas fetch. It then blends the three frames weighted by coverage, decodes sRGB (`sRGBTransferEOTF()`), and multiplies by the instance tint: with `SCENERY_PALETTE`, `mix(vTint, vPaintTint, mask)`, where the mask is the blend of the premultiplied `uImpostorPaint` from the same frames divided by their coverage (three more fetches, one with `IMPOSTOR_SINGLE_FRAME`). It then applies the `uAtmosphere` clamp (`700`→`400`). The same coverage-weighted blend averages the baked depth, which moves the flat quad position (`vShadowPosition`) along the flat view direction (`vShadowView`) to the surface (`impostorShadowPosition`) for the [scenery shadow](#scenery-shadows).
  - `IMPOSTOR_SINGLE_FRAME`, set on mobile, samples only the dominant frame.
  - Coverage goes to `diffuseColor.a`, and Three.js's `alphatest_fragment` turns it into MSAA coverage (`alphaToCoverage`, threshold `0.5`).
  - `impostor-normal-fragment.glsl` replaces `normal_fragment_begin`. It rebuilds the view-space normal from the baked normal through the per-instance yaw, stretch, and bend basis.
- **GLSL twins:** [`impostor-octahedral.glsl`](../src/shaders/impostor-octahedral.glsl) mirrors `octahedral.js`: `encodeImpostorView()` and `decodeImpostorView()` map directions to grid positions in `[0, 1]²` for the layout its defines select. With `IMPOSTOR_LOWER_HEMISPHERE` the hemi-octahedral mapping is mirrored on y (`hemisphere = -1` in JavaScript). The frame grid and atlas UVs use `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES_X` and `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES_Y`. Keep the frame basis identical to the baker, which uses `camera.lookAt()` with world up; seen from below it still keeps world up on screen, and neither even hemi-octahedral counts nor the frontal band bake the degenerate pole.
- **Known limits:**
  - The quad is flat, so a large instance on a steep slope can be partially hidden by terrain in front of its center.
  - Adjacent frames can ghost slightly where silhouettes differ.
  - Distant mips lose coverage on thin parts.
  - The baked depth channel is also a candidate for a `gl_FragDepth` correction; today only the shadow lookup reads it.
  - Up close, parts of the object in front of or behind the quad plane appear displaced, and the three-frame blend ghosts. The [near meshes](#near-scenery-meshes) replace impostors before this becomes large.

### Scenery Palettes

The trees' and palms' crowns, the whole cacti, the whole rocks (boulders, layered rocks, and sea rocks), and the whole ice spikes take one color per instance from a palette, picked on the GPU, so one atlas serves every color. The [clouds](#clouds) use the same machinery with a palette of their own. The pure rules and defaults are in [`src/sceneryPalettePolicy.js`](../src/sceneryPalettePolicy.js).

- **Palettes:** `SCENERY_PALETTE_TYPES` maps each painted type to a palette and a noise group: `roundTree` and `conifer` (group `trees`), one `cactus` palette shared by both cactus types (group `cacti`), so a region paints both alike, and `boulder`, `layeredRock` (group `rocks`), `seaRock` (group `seaRocks`), and one `iceSpike` palette shared by both ice spike types (group `iceSpikes`), which pick their color by biome, then `palm` (group `palms`). `SCENERY_PALETTE_KEYS` lists the palettes in type order; the first noise palettes come first and new ones go last, so their indices (and noise fields) never move.
- **Settings:** `params.sceneryPalette` (`createSceneryPaletteSettings()`) holds `noise` (per group, `frequency` per world unit and `mix`, every group `0.05` and `0.9`) and `palettes` (`colors` as `{ label, color, weight }` in sRGB hex, plus a `trunk` color for the trees and the palm; cacti and rocks are painted whole). A palette with `byBiome` (the rocks) uses neither noise nor weights: its colors paint the temperate biome, the desert, the ice, and the deep ocean (`SCENERY_BIOME_SLOTS`, the four slots a palette has), so its group has no `noise` entry. The defaults:

  | Palette | Trunk | Colors |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `roundTree` | `#7a4a2a` | light wood `#b88347`, light green wood `#a4ad38`, orange wood `#ff674d`, dark brown wood `#257960` |
  | `conifer` | `#5e3a22` | dark wood `#2e856f`, dark green wood `#5a731c`, bluish green wood `#105c70` |
  | `cactus` | none | brown wood `#8f5f3a` (the former cactus color), light green wood `#a3c46b`, yellow wood `#e8c04a` |
  | `palm` | `#eb7047` | green wood `#588d59`, lime green wood `#afda52`, teal green wood `#58ad88`, yellow green wood `#987e1f` (weight `0.5`) |
  | `boulder` (by biome) | none | forest `#c7b18e`, desert `#deb583`, ice `#d3e2ec`, deep ocean `#b9a690` (unused) |
  | `layeredRock` (by biome) | none | forest `#e3c9a0`, desert `#e3c9a0`, ice `#dce8f0`, deep ocean `#e3c9a0` (unused) |
  | `seaRock` (by biome) | none | forest `#5f3b2b`, desert `#e27865`, ice `#8fa9bd`, deep ocean `#70605c` (warm grey) |
  | `iceSpike` (by biome) | none | `#c6ecfa` in every slot (the spikes stand only in the ice) |

  Weights are relative shares (`1` each); `0` disables a slot. The cacti keep their faint, non-normalized wood detail, so the brown slot reproduces the former cactus exactly. The forest and desert rock colors reproduce the former wood colors times the former placement tints (boulders greyed in the forest and warmed in the desert), so only the ice frosts them. The layered rocks no longer stand in the ice, so their ice color is unused.
- **Uniforms:** `createSceneryPaletteUniforms()` (`impostorMaterial.js`) makes `uSceneryPaletteColors[IMPOSTOR_TYPE_COUNT * SCENERY_PALETTE_SIZE]`, `uSceneryPaletteWeights[IMPOSTOR_TYPE_COUNT]` (one `vec4` per type, so `SCENERY_PALETTE_SIZE` is `4`), `uSceneryTrunkColors[IMPOSTOR_TYPE_COUNT]`, and `uSceneryPaletteNoise[IMPOSTOR_TYPE_COUNT]` (frequency, mix, palette index, and `1` for a palette picked by biome). `SceneryImpostors` owns them and shares them with the impostor material (inside `impostorUniforms`, so the wireframe twin has them) and the near meshes. `applyPalette()` writes `getSceneryPaletteLayout()` into them in place through `setSceneryPaletteUniforms()`: `Color.set()` converts the hex to linear, then the color is divided by `SCENERY_PAINT_BASE` to undo the gray bake. Unpainted types, unused slots, and the cacti's trunk stay white with zero weights; a painted type whose weights are all `0` keeps slot `0`. Materials created without them get neutral defaults, because a program that declares a uniform must receive it.
- **Choice (`getSceneryPaletteTints()` in `scenery-instance-pars-vertex.glsl`):** at `(base.xz + uBiomeOffset) × frequency` (so each world seed paints its own map), every slot with a positive weight samples its own `snoise` field, offset per palette and slot inside the noise's `289`-unit period, mapped to `[0, 1]`; types that share a palette share its fields. The field is mixed by `mix` with a per-instance hash of the yaw bits and slot (integer math, so the impostor and mesh programs agree). The slot with the largest `log(value) / weight` wins, a weighted race in which each slot's share follows its weight. `mix 0` gives clean patches; `mix 1` gives every instance a random color.
- **By biome:** painted types vary in brightness only, so placement stores the biome's palette slot in the blue byte of their packed tint (`getPaintedTint()` in [`src/sceneryPlacement.js`](../src/sceneryPlacement.js)), and `getSceneryPaletteTints()` (which takes the packed value too) uses the red channel as the brightness of every painted type. For a `byBiome` palette it skips the noise and reads the slot, so a rock takes the color of the biome placement chose for it, without evaluating the biome per vertex; the scenery programs no longer include `biome-value.glsl`. Land rocks stand off the biome borders (placement margins), so they match the terrain under them; a sea rock group takes its first rock's biome, so a group right on a border keeps one color.
- **Clouds:** `params.clouds.palette` (`createCloudPaletteSettings()`) is one palette shared by every cloud type, painted whole (no trunk): `frequency` `0.002` and `mix` `0.5` distribute white wood `#ffffff`, cyan wood `#61fcff`, slate blue wood `#5e68b0`, and azure wood `#009dff` (weight `1` each). `getCloudPaletteLayout()` repeats it for every cloud type, with the noise fields of `CLOUD_PALETTE_INDEX` (`16`, past the scenery palettes', so the sky never repeats a ground palette's map). `Clouds` owns its own `createSceneryPaletteUniforms(CLOUD_TYPE_COUNT)` and writes them with `setSceneryPaletteUniforms()` in `applyPalette()`. Cloud placement packs a gray tint, so the red channel (the brightness) is all the palette reads.
- **Both draws agree:** the impostor and the near meshes compute the choice from the same flat world base, yaw, and uniforms, and mix the trunk and painted tints by the same mask (baked mask for the impostor, `paint` attribute for the mesh), so the dithered hand-over shows no color change.
- **GUI:** **Biomes > Forest > Tree palette**, **Biomes > Desert > Cactus palette**, and **Biomes > Deep ocean > Palm palette** set their group's patch frequency and random mix, and each palette's colors and weights (and the trees' and palm's trunk colors); each biome's **Rock colors** folder sets its boulder and sea rock colors, plus the layered rock's in the desert and the ice spikes' in the ice (the deep ocean's only its sea rocks'). **Clouds > Palette** sets the cloud palette's patch frequency, random mix, colors, and weights. All apply live. Shadows are unaffected (they read only coverage).
- **Cost:** up to four `snoise` per near-mesh vertex and per impostor vertex of a tree, palm, cactus, or cloud (none for the rocks), three more atlas fetches per impostor fragment, and `vPaintTint`, which brings the impostor to 14 varying rows of the 15 WebGL2 guarantees. `pnpm bench --compare` showed no measurable change on an Apple Silicon Mac.

### Near Scenery Meshes

Close to the eye, scenery instances are drawn as their real source meshes in two levels of detail instead of impostors. [`src/impostors/sceneryMeshes.js`](../src/impostors/sceneryMeshes.js) owns them; [`src/sceneryMeshPolicy.js`](../src/sceneryMeshPolicy.js) holds the pure fade, range, and selection rules. The class takes a `catalog` (scenery by default), so the clouds use a second instance with `CLOUD_IMPOSTORS`; the rules below apply to both.

- **Levels:** LOD 0 is the geometry the impostor was baked from. LOD 1 (`createScenerySources(type, 2)` in `impostorArchetypes.js`) builds the same parts with fewer segments, shifted by the LOD 0 recentering so both share one local frame. LOD 1 triangles: 364 round tree, 78 conifer, 170 and 250 for the one- and two-arm cacti (a plain 9-sided trunk without ribs), 180 boulder, 112 layered rock, 108 sea rock, 60 and 90 for the two- and three-spike ice spikes, and 396 palm (flat fronds without the V-fold). The boat's two levels come from its model (16,000 and 3,000 triangles, see **Boat sources** under [Impostor Scenery](#impostor-scenery)).
- **Color maps:** the meshes share each source's `position`, `normal`, `color`, `paint`, and, when it has one, `uv`. A source with `userData.map` (the boat) is drawn with a per-level copy of the level's material whose `map` is that texture; Three.js multiplies the color by the map in `map_fragment`, before the patched `color_fragment`, so the shared shader is unchanged. The copies count as separate programs (`USE_MAP`); the wireframe twin stays the level's.
- **Bands:** `params.sceneryMeshes` (`createSceneryMeshSettings()`) sets `enabled`, `lodStart`, `lodEnd`, `start`, and `end`, in units from the eye (`cameraPosition`) to the instance's flat world base.

  | Range | Desktop | Mobile | Hand-over |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `lodStart → lodEnd` | `110 → 150` | `60 → 90` | LOD 0 to LOD 1 |
  | `start → end` | `220 → 300` | `120 → 180` | LOD 1 to impostor |

  `SceneryMeshes.applySettings()` writes the shared `uSceneryMeshRange` uniform (`getSceneryMeshRange()`) and its own `uSceneryMeshLodRange` (`getSceneryMeshLodRange()`). Each band keeps `end ≥ start + 1`, and the LOD band is clamped inside the mesh range. While disabled, both are `(-2, -1)`, so the mesh fade is `0` everywhere and impostors render exactly as before.
- **Cross-fade:**
  - Every shader computes `meshFade = 1 - smoothstep(start, end, distance)` per instance. The mesh shader also computes `lodFade = min(1 - smoothstep(lodStart, lodEnd, distance), meshFade)`.
  - [`scenery-dither-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-dither-pars-fragment.glsl) gives each pixel one interleaved-gradient-noise value `n`, shifted per instance by its yaw.
  - LOD 0 keeps `n < lodFade`, LOD 1 keeps `lodFade ≤ n < meshFade`, and the impostor keeps `n ≥ meshFade`. Each pixel therefore shows exactly one of the three, with no blending, sorting, or double coverage.
  - A fully faded impostor collapses its quad. A mesh instance whose level has no share collapses to its base, so its triangles have zero area.
- **Matching the impostor:**
  - The vertex shader ([`scenery-mesh-normal-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-mesh-normal-vertex.glsl), replacing `beginnormal_vertex`, and [`scenery-mesh-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-mesh-vertex.glsl), replacing `project_vertex`) applies stretch, scale, yaw, a floating boat's tilt onto the water ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)), and the rigid bend at the curved base. This is the inverse of the impostor's world-to-baked-frame transform. Normals use the inverse-transpose stretch, yaw, and bend.
  - [`scenery-mesh-color-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-mesh-color-fragment.glsl) multiplies the vertex color by the shared wood detail in object space (with the type's `vSceneryDetail`), then by the instance tint and position variation: with `SCENERY_PALETTE`, `mix(vTint, vPaintTint, vSceneryPaint)`, the same trunk and painted tints as the impostor, mixed by the source's `paint`. It applies the impostor's atmosphere clamp (`700 → 400`).
  - Lighting uses `roughness 0.9`, `metalness 0`, the curved terminator, and a view-space `vSphereNormal`, as the impostor does. Vertex colors stay linear; the bake's sRGB encoding is undone by the impostor.
  - In the LOD 1 to impostor band, both are small on screen and their silhouettes agree closely; LOD 1 crowns are slightly smoother. LOD 1 facets become visible close up, which is why LOD 0 takes over in the nearer band.
  - Moving the impostor band much closer shows the quad's flat-plane parallax and frame ghosting as a dithered double outline.
- **Draws:**
  - Each level has one material, compiled with `SCENERY_MESH_LOD` `0` or `1`, and one `InstancedBufferGeometry` per type: at most two draw calls per type (`22`), and only for types with near instances.
  - Each geometry shares its source index and its `position`, `normal`, `color`, and (scenery only) `paint` attributes. It adds its own `DynamicDrawUsage` instance buffer in the 8-float placement layout, with a world-space base.
  - Meshes sit at the world origin with frustum culling disabled; a mesh with no instances is hidden.
- **Selection (CPU, every frame):** `update()` runs in `World.tic()` (through `SceneryImpostors.update()`) after `ChunkManager.updateChunks()` and before rendering, so scenery committed this frame is already included. It is built on `beginUpdate(camera)`, `appendInstances(instances, x, y, z)`, and `endUpdate()`, which the cloud field calls directly with its single world-space array.
  - `getSceneryMeshSelection()` gives each level a distance window: LOD 0 from `0` to `lodEnd + 2`, LOD 1 from `lodStart - 2` to `end + 2`.
  - The update visits live chunks with scenery whose square lies within the outer window of the eye, and reads their instance arrays without copying.
  - It tests each instance's curved bounding sphere against the camera frustum at most once, then appends it to every level whose window holds it. Instances in the LOD band go to both levels.
  - Per-type arrays double when full and are otherwise reused, and only the used range is uploaded.
  - The windows cover every distance where a level has a share, and the GPU computes the fades from the same eye, so every visible pixel share has its mesh.
- **Wireframe overlay:** see [Scenery Wireframe Overlay](#scenery-wireframe-overlay).
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getSceneryMeshStats()` reports `enabled`, `range`, `lodRange`, `instances`, `triangles`, `drawCalls`, and `updateMs`. Under `levels`, it reports for each level its `window`, `instances`, and `triangles`, and per type `instances`, `capacity`, `reallocations`, and `sourceTriangles`.

### Scenery Wireframe Overlay

A debug view that outlines the scenery triangles in one flat color per level of detail. [`src/impostors/sceneryWireframe.js`](../src/impostors/sceneryWireframe.js) owns the material patch; `params.sceneryWireframe` (`createSceneryWireframeSettings()` in [`src/sceneryMeshPolicy.js`](../src/sceneryMeshPolicy.js)) holds `enabled` (default `false`), `meshColors` (LOD 0 `#ff4d4d`, LOD 1 `#ffd23f`), and `impostorColor` (`#3fd5ff`).

- **Twins:** every near-mesh level has a second material from the same `createMaterial()`, and `createImpostorWireframeMaterial()` builds one from the impostor material. Each twin draws a mesh that shares its solid mesh's geometry with `wireframe: true` and `renderOrder` `1`. The impostor twin is a child of each chunk's scenery mesh and shares the impostor's `impostorUniforms`, so a re-bake reaches both; it never owns the atlas. Near-mesh twins follow their solid mesh's visibility every frame.
- **Shader:** `makeSceneryWireframeMaterial()` adds the `SCENERY_WIREFRAME` define (the twins share their solid material's `onBeforeCompile` source, so without it they would reuse its program), drops the impostor's `alphaTest` and `alphaToCoverage` so quad edges are not discarded, and stores the `uSceneryWireframeColor` uniform. `patchSceneryWireframeShader()` inserts [`scenery-wireframe-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-wireframe-fragment.glsl) after `opaque_fragment`: the flat color replaces the shaded one (fog and tone mapping still apply), and the fragment depth moves toward the eye by `0.1%` of the distance (`gl_FragCoord.z - 1e-3 * (1 - gl_FragCoord.z)`, since `1 - depth` is about `near / distance`) so edges do not z-fight their own surface. Polygon offset would not work: it does not apply to lines. Writing the depth costs early depth rejection, but only for the overlay.
- **Coverage:** the twins keep the vertex collapse and the dither discard, so in each cross-fade band an edge pixel shows only the level that draws it, and the dither breaks the lines into dots.
- **Toggle:** `setSceneryWireframe()` sets the twin material's `visible` and color. Hidden materials are skipped by the renderer, so the disabled overlay costs only the twins' scene traversal and the impostor twins' frustum tests. The GUI and `SceneryMeshes.applyWireframe()` apply changes live.

### Scenery Shadows

Scenery (trees, cacti, rocks), the airplane, and the terrain cast soft shadows on the terrain and on the scenery. [`src/sceneryShadows.js`](../src/sceneryShadows.js) owns the pass; [`src/shadowPolicy.js`](../src/shadowPolicy.js) holds the pure rules and defaults (`createSceneryShadowSettings()`, `params.shadows`). Three.js's own shadow maps are not used.

- **Flat world space:** casters and receivers use positions before the visual curvature and bend. The shadow maps therefore need no curvature. At the fade distance the bend is about `0.1`–`0.15` rad, and shadows there are already soft and faint.
- **Light:** `selectShadowLight()` picks the sun or the moon, whichever has the higher flat elevation (`asin(direction.y)`). The strength is multiplied by `smoothstep(0.05, 0.2, elevation)`, because grazing light casts very long shadows. At the sun/moon hand-over both lights are low, so the strength is `0` and the switch cannot pop. `uSceneryShadowStrength = strength × params.shadows.strength` (default `0.8`). When it is `0` or `enabled` is off, the pass is skipped and every receiver returns early; with `enabled` off the terrain is currently not drawn at all ([`CORR-003`](ROADMAP.md#known-problem-register)). The glare takes its own share, `params.shadows.specular` (default `1`): `uSceneryShadowSpecular = specular / strength` (`getSpecularShadowScale()`, `0` while the shadows are off) turns a lookup into the same shadow with that share in place of the strength, without a second lookup, so a full shadow removes all the glare ([Curved-World Lighting](#curved-world-lighting)).
- **Cascades:** two `OrthographicCamera`s aligned to the light, each rendering into a `WebGLRenderTarget` with a `DepthTexture` (`UnsignedShortType`, so `DEPTH_COMPONENT16`, with `LessEqualCompare` and `LinearFilter`). The orthographic depth is linear and the far cascade spans about `1100` units (`2100` at the GUI's largest radius), so one step (`0.017` to `0.032` units) stays below the default `0.05` depth bias. Casters never write color, but a render target needs a color attachment, so it is a single 8-bit channel (`RedFormat`). Every `sampler2DShadow` tap is a bilinear hardware comparison.

  | Cascade | Desktop radius / map / interval | Mobile radius / map / interval |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Near | `150` u / `2048²` / every frame | `120` u / `1024²` / every frame |
  | Far | `500` u / `2048²` / every 2 frames | `400` u / `1024²` / every 3 frames |

  - Each cascade covers a disk of terrain of its radius, centered `forwardShift × radius` ahead of the plane along its heading (`0.35` near, `0.25` far).
  - The camera box is a square of half-size `√(radius² + (heightRange / 2)²)`, the sphere around that disk over `heightRange` (`-2` to `180`). Its size does not depend on the light direction.
  - The camera sits `casterMargin` (`60`) beyond the sphere toward the light, so casters above the height range still render. Terrain casters farther toward the light are flattened onto the near plane (see the terrain casters below).
  - The center is snapped to whole texels in light space. The light basis is rebuilt only after the light turns more than `lightThreshold` (`0.0035` rad), so the texel grid stays fixed between those steps and edges do not shimmer. The light-space math is shared with the cloud shadows in [`src/lightSpace.js`](../src/lightSpace.js): `LightBasis` (direction, basis, and `fit()`, which places an orthographic camera around a sphere with its center snapped to texels), `writeShadowMatrix()`, `createImpostorCasterMaterial()`, and `createTerrainCasterMaterial()`.
  - Cascades with an interval above 1 render on staggered frames. Each keeps the matrix it was rendered with, so an older far map still lines up in world space. A new light basis or `applySettings()` renders every cascade.
- **Casters:** a private `Scene` holds one pooled proxy `Mesh` per live scenery chunk within the far sphere. Each proxy reuses the chunk's impostor `InstancedBufferGeometry` at the chunk position. The renderer frustum-culls the proxies per cascade through the geometry's bounding sphere. There is no per-instance CPU work. An airplane proxy draws the simplified shadow caster (`shadowPath` in [`src/airplaneModels.js`](../src/airplaneModels.js), about `4,000` triangles instead of `183,000` for the biplane, see [Assets](ASSETS.md#encoding-pipeline)), placed in the model's geometry space like the model, and copies the plane mesh's `matrixWorld`; without it (load failure) it falls back to the full mesh. It is drawn only in the near cascade. Under the penumbra the simplified shadow matches the full mesh within `8` levels of `255`.
  - [`scenery-shadow-caster-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-shadow-caster-vertex.glsl) builds each instance as a quad facing the light through its bounding-sphere center, without curvature, bend, or mesh fade. It picks the three baked frames around the light direction with `selectImpostorFrames()`, shared with the impostor, and gets each frame UV from `getFrameUv()` with the viewer placed far along the light, which is an exact orthographic projection.
  - [`scenery-shadow-caster-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-shadow-caster-fragment.glsl) discards texels whose blended atlas coverage is below `0.5` (dominant frame only with `IMPOSTOR_SINGLE_FRAME`). Both casters write depth only (`colorWrite: false`, `DoubleSide`).
  - The impostor caster shares the `uImpostorAlbedo` and `uImpostorTypes` uniform objects of the impostor material, so a re-bake reaches it.
  - **Terrain casters:** the terrain casts so that scenery shadows do not float past a hill's own shadow, for example over the sea behind it. Before each cascade renders, `syncTerrainCasters()` points a pool of proxies (`frustumCulled = false`, `renderOrder = -1`, so terrain depth is written before the impostors' discarding casters) at the chunks that can shadow that cascade. Each proxy draws the chunk's shadow view for that cascade ([Terrain](TERRAIN.md#height-generation)): a coarse grid over the chunk's own vertices, with no new vertex data and no upload. `params.shadows.terrainCasters.segments` is `[64, 32]` on desktop (cells of `4` and `8` units) and `[32, 16]` on mobile, and a chunk whose own grid is coarser keeps it (`getTerrainShadowSegments()`). An edge facing a coarser neighbor is stitched to it (`getShadowEdgeFactor()`), so long shadows show no light lines at chunk borders.
    - **Back faces only:** [`terrain-shadow-caster-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/terrain-shadow-caster-vertex.glsl) draws with `BackSide`, so only the faces turned away from the light write depth. A ray toward the light can enter a height field only through such a face, so these faces alone give the terrain's shadows. Lit slopes and the flat sea never shadow themselves (no acne, so the terrain receiver keeps a `selfBias` of `0`). Faces turned away from the light receive no direct light anyway. The vertex moves `terrainCasters.offset` (`0.5`) units away from the light, a depth bias for the terrain alone that covers the gap between the coarse grid and the drawn grid without moving tree shadows.
    - **Pancaking:** the same shader clamps `gl_Position.z` to the near plane, so terrain upstream of the cascade camera still shadows it. Every receiver lies at least `casterMargin` beyond the near plane, deeper than a clamped grid cell can reach.
    - **Selection** (pure rules in [`src/shadowPolicy.js`](../src/shadowPolicy.js)): a chunk casts into a cascade when its footprint meets the cascade disk swept toward the light by its reach (`terrainChunkCastsIntoDisk()`). The reach (`getTerrainCasterReach()`) is how far upstream its highest point (from the flat sphere's height range) can still shadow the lowest receiver (`heightRange.min`), capped by `terrainCasters.maxReach` (`500` on desktop, `350` on mobile). The same test runs against the fade disk around the plane, since receivers beyond `fade.end` get no shadow. `terrainChunkFacesAwayFrom()` skips chunks no steeper (`maxSlope` from the worker) than the light's elevation: they have no face turned away from the light, so open sea and gentle land cost no draw call, and most chunks drop out when the sun is high.
- **Receivers:** [`scenery-shadow-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-shadow-pars-fragment.glsl) provides `getSceneryShadow(position, selfBias)`, the visibility in `[0, 1]`. Each receiver passes its PCF sample counts to `createSceneryLighting()`, which sets them as the defines `SCENERY_SHADOW_TAPS_NEAR` and `SCENERY_SHADOW_TAPS_FAR` from `getSceneryShadowTapDefines()` in [`src/shadowPolicy.js`](../src/shadowPolicy.js) (`1` to `SCENERY_SHADOW_MAX_TAPS`, `16`). As defines they are compile-time loop bounds and part of the program cache key, so receivers with different counts never share a program. Unshadowed materials (`SCENERY_NO_SHADOWS`, the clouds) include the chunk without them and get only its declarations.
  1. Distance fade from the plane (`uCamera`, horizontal): full below `fade.start` and none beyond `fade.end` (desktop `220 → 450`, mobile `180 → 360`). Beyond it the function returns before sampling.
  2. Cascade weights from the horizontal distance to each cascade center. The near weight falls over `0.75`–`0.95` of its radius, and both cascades are sampled and mixed in that band. The far cascade fades to lit over `0.85`–`0.98` of its radius. No transition has a hard edge.
  3. Penumbra radius in world units from `softness.near` (`0.6`) to `softness.far` (`2.5`) by distance, converted to UV per cascade. The edge stays soft and consistent across cascades and widens far away.
  4. A Vogel disk of `taps` samples, rotated per pixel by interleaved gradient noise. The offsets come from a constant table (`SCENERY_SHADOW_SPIRAL`: tap `i` at angle `i × 2.39996323` and radius `√(i + 0.5)`, divided by `√taps`), and one rotation matrix per pixel serves both cascades, so no tap evaluates a trigonometric function; a test checks the table against the formula. One tap samples the center. The receiver depth is moved toward the light by `bias` (`0.05` units) plus `selfBias`.

  The shadowed lighting from `createSceneryLighting()` computes `sceneryShadow` once per fragment, as `getSceneryShadow(...) * getCloudShadow(...)` at the same flat position (see [Cloud Shadows](#cloud-shadows)), and multiplies only the direct light of the shadowing light, after the curved terminator: `step(0, dot(directLight.direction, light))`. Sun and moon are always opposite, so this test picks the light without depending on Three's light order. The same light's glare follows `sceneryShadowSpecular`. `SceneryShadows.update()` writes that light to `uSceneryShadowLight` every frame, even when its own strength is `0`, because the cloud shadows dim the same light. Ambient light is never shadowed.

  | Receiver | Flat position | `selfBias` | Taps desktop / mobile |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Terrain | `vShadowPosition`, the chunk-space vertex through `modelMatrix` before waves and curvature (`wPosition.y` is later overwritten with `height`) | `0` | near `8` / far `4`; `4` / `2` |
  | Near meshes | instance base plus yawed, stretched, scaled vertex, without bend | LOD 0 bounding radius (`uSceneryBoundRadius`) × scale × stretch | `4`; `2` |
  | Impostors | quad position with the bend undone, moved to the surface by the baked depth | bounding-sphere radius (frame radius × scale × stretch) | `2`; `1` |

  The scenery self-bias of about one instance radius keeps every instance out of its own flat caster quad, which would otherwise split it at the quad plane. Scenery therefore receives shadows only from other casters (neighbors, the airplane, and the terrain); its own shading still comes from `N·L` and the baked vertical occlusion. An instance on a slope turned away from the light is darkened by the terrain only where the slope rises farther than that bias toward the light, so trees just below a crest stay lit.
- **Frame order:** `World.tic()` calls `SceneryShadows.update()` after `SceneryImpostors.update()` and before `PostProcessing.render()`. It saves and restores the renderer's render target and clears only the depth of each rendered cascade.
- **Cost:** about 5–25 instanced quad draws per rendered cascade, plus the airplane caster (about 4,000 triangles) in the near cascade, which cut the frame's triangles from about `0.99` M to `0.81` M; no per-frame allocation. On desktop, two `2048²` targets with 16-bit depth and the `R8` color attachment a render target needs take about `25` MB; on mobile, a quarter of that. Terrain pixels beyond the fade cost one uniform branch.
  - Terrain casters, in the default bench flight on the development machine: on desktop, 4–8 chunks in the near cascade (`8,192` triangles each) and 12–17 in the far one (`2,048` each); on the mobile preset, about 5 (`2,048` each) and 12 (`512` each). Front faces are culled before rasterization, and receivers cost nothing more. `pnpm bench --compare` against the build without them measured `+0.3%` mean frame time at `3840 × 2160` (default query and `time=0.25`), `+0.6%` at `1920 × 1080` (`--dpr 1`), and `+1.0%` with the mobile preset (`--width 700 --height 1400`). At `3840 × 2160` with a low sun, the frames over `35` ms (the GPU-bound pipeline stalling twice) went from 13–16 to 21–23 of 600.
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getShadowStats()` reports `enabled`, `light`, `lightElevation`, `strength`, `specularScale`, `casters`, `airplane`, `airplaneTriangles` (the caster drawn), `terrainCasters` (the settings), `drawCalls`, and `updateMs` (CPU), and for each cascade `radius`, `mapSize`, `interval`, `center`, `lastFrame`, `drawCalls`, `triangles` (both of its last render), and the terrain casters selected at its last render: `terrainCasters` (chunks), `terrainTriangles`, and `stitched` (chunks with a snapped edge).
- **GUI:** the **Shadows** folder (`?gui=1`) sets `enabled`, `strength`, **Glare removed** (`specular`), softness near and far, fade start and end, depth bias, and both cascade radii live; its **Terrain casters** folder sets `enabled`, `offset`, and `maxReach` live, and **Low sun (terrain)** the [low-sun occlusion](#low-sun-terrain-occlusion). Map sizes, intervals, tap counts, and the terrain shadow grids are fixed at startup.
- **Known limits:** each caster is a flat quad at its center depth, so a receiver point within about one instance radius of another caster's center plane is treated as lit. Near shadows come from impostor silhouettes (64 px frames), not from the real meshes. Terrain shadows end with every other shadow at `fade.end`, and their reach upstream at `maxReach` or at the edge of the loaded chunks. Their outlines follow the coarse shadow grid (`4` to `8` units on desktop) under the penumbra.

### Clouds

Clouds are carved-wood slabs in the style of [`docs/style-references/cloud-reference.png`](style-references/cloud-reference.png), drawn through the impostor pipeline with `CLOUD_IMPOSTORS`. [`src/clouds.js`](../src/clouds.js) owns them; placement is in [Terrain](TERRAIN.md#clouds).

- **Sources:** [`src/impostors/cloudArchetypes.js`](../src/impostors/cloudArchetypes.js) builds four types (`CLOUD_TYPE`): `BANK`, `HEAP`, and `PUFF` as two slabs each, and `TOWER` as three slabs that climb toward the back (a low front slab, a taller one behind it to the right, the tallest at the back on the left), the tallest and deepest cloud. A slab is a flat cloud outline extruded along Z with a `1.6`-unit bevel. The outline is a flat base and a chain of circular bumps whose notches and bottom corners are rounded with quadratic fillets. The base edge is subdivided so the rounded corners' smooth normals do not tint the whole underside. Caps keep their exact `±Z` normal, while the bevel and side wall share smooth normals; each slab behind sits behind and to the side of the one in front. Clouds are painted whole like the cacti: every slab is baked in the neutral gray `SCENERY_PAINT_BASE` (the slabs behind at `0.92` and `0.85` of it) with a `paint` mask of `1`, and the [cloud palette](#scenery-palettes) colors them at runtime, so the palette color is the front face's. Base on `y = 0`, bounding sphere on the Y axis. LOD 0 has about 4,350, 3,770, 2,870, and 5,450 triangles; LOD 1 about 1,040, 890, 680, and 1,280.
- **Facing the airplane:** every cloud turns about its vertical axis so its front face (local `-Z`, the front slab) looks at the airplane. With `SCENERY_FACE_AIRPLANE`, the impostor and near-mesh shaders compute the yaw from the cloud's flat world base and `uCamera` (the airplane) with `getFacingYaw()` ([`scenery-facing.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-facing.glsl), twin of `getFacingYaw()` in `cloudPlacement.js`). Both use the same inputs, so the LOD hand-over stays seamless. With the airplane straight below, the cloud keeps its starting orientation. The yaw slot only seeds the dither, so every cloud keeps its own pattern. A cloud passing close beside or over the airplane turns quickly to keep facing it.
- **Frontal views:** clouds face the airplane and float above it, so the camera sees them only from the front and below. Their atlas bakes a frontal band (`CLOUD_IMPOSTOR_VIEWS`, `IMPOSTOR_FRONTAL_VIEWS`): `3` azimuths within `±10°` of the front face and `10` elevations from the horizon down to `75°` below it, `30` views per type instead of `144`. Beyond the near meshes the follow camera stays within about `4°` of the front and `10°`–`40°` below the horizon; the margins keep other band settings plausible. Frames are `96` px on desktop and `64` on mobile (the baker can shrink them), in `4 × 1` type blocks: `1152 × 960` (about `13` MB with mips) and `768 × 640` (about `6` MB). With three types, the former lower-hemisphere grid took `3456 × 1152` (about `42` MB) and `2304 × 768` (about `19` MB).
- **Material:** one impostor material without shadows and with `translucent` (below), with `singleFrame` on mobile, its own `uImpostorFarFade` (`getCloudFarFade(radius)`: `1600 → 1800` on desktop, `850 → 1050` on mobile), and its own `uSceneryMeshRange`, held in a copy of the shared uniform object. The near meshes (`SceneryMeshes` with `CLOUD_IMPOSTORS`, `receiveShadows: false`, `translucent: true`) share that copy.
- **Look:** the wood detail is baked normalized, from `params.clouds.detail` (`scale 0.012`, `color 0.6`), the same setting for all four types (`Clouds.applyDetail()` writes it into the near meshes' `uSceneryDetail` with `normalized` on), so the grain keeps the palette color. The ambient light is multiplied by `params.clouds.ambient` (`2.72`), because the camera mostly sees undersides and faces turned away from the sun. Clouds skip the atmosphere clamp, so only fog fades them. Each cloud takes one color from the cloud palette (white, cyan, slate blue, or azure wood by default, see [Scenery Palettes](#scenery-palettes)), and each type has a brightness variation (`params.clouds.variation`: `bank 0.99`, `heap 1.26`, `puff 0.99`, `tower 1.1` stops, frequency `0.0405`).
- **Translucency:** both levels compile `CLOUD_TRANSLUCENCY`, and the unshadowed light loop adds `getCloudTranslucency()` ([`cloud-translucency-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/cloud-translucency-pars-fragment.glsl)) to the direct diffuse of each directional light, after the curved terminator: the light's color × the albedo's Lambert term × the tint × `strength` × a forward lobe `pow(max(dot(−view, light), 0), focus)`, strongest with the light right behind the cloud, × a thickness `mix(body, 1, pow(1 − |N·V|, rim))`, so the bevels and silhouettes seen edge-on carry the most light (the silver lining) and the caps glow at `body` of it. The moon carries `moon` of the sun's share, told apart by `uSunDirection`. Defaults (`params.clouds.translucency`): strength `1.2`, focus `4`, body `0.35`, rim `2`, moon `0.5`, tint `#ffe6c8`; **Clouds > Translucency** edits them live through `Clouds.applyAppearance()`. It adds no varying (the impostor uses `14` of `15`); impostors light it from their baked normals, so the hand-over keeps their existing normal mismatch. The cloud shadow caster compiles without it.
- **Bands:** `params.clouds.meshes` (`createCloudMeshSettings()`, `CLOUD_MESH_RANGES`), measured to the cloud's flat base:

  | Range | Desktop | Mobile | Hand-over |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `lodStart → lodEnd` | `220 → 300` | `120 → 180` | LOD 0 to LOD 1 |
  | `start → end` | `700 → 850` | `380 → 480` | LOD 1 to impostor |

  The impostor takes over only where its frames match the screen resolution.
- **Draws:** one impostor draw for the whole field (the mesh sits at the origin with world-space bases and frustum culling disabled), plus at most eight near-mesh draws (one per type and level).
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getCloudStats()` reports `instances`, `cell`, `revision`, `regenerateMs`, the atlas `views` (`[framesX, framesY]`), `frameSize`, `width`, and `height`, and the near-mesh stats under `meshes`.
- **Known limits:** the `?debug=1` orbit camera can look at far clouds from outside the frontal band (from above, behind, or the side); their impostors then show the nearest band-edge frame. Clouds face the airplane, not the orbit camera. The near meshes are correct from any side.

### Cloud Shadows

Clouds cast soft shadows on the terrain and the scenery. [`src/cloudShadows.js`](../src/cloudShadows.js) owns the pass; the pure rules and defaults are in [`src/shadowPolicy.js`](../src/shadowPolicy.js) (`createCloudShadowSettings()`, `params.clouds.shadows`).

- **Coverage map:** every cloud floats above every receiver (cloud bases from Y `197`, terrain peaks below about `95`), so no depth comparison is needed. The map stores, for each light ray, how much of it a cloud covers. The receiver test reads only that value at the receiver's position projected along the light.
- **Render:** an `OrthographicCamera` aligned to the light covers the disk of `radius` around the airplane (desktop `1100` u on a `1024²` map, mobile `800` u on `512²`), over the receiver heights `-2` to `120`. Its depth range is `±8000`, so every cloud along a light ray renders, even with a low light. The center is snapped to whole texels in light space.
- **Caster:** one proxy draws the cloud field's impostor geometry with [`scenery-shadow-caster-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-shadow-caster-vertex.glsl) (material from `createImpostorCasterMaterial()`). Under `SCENERY_FACE_AIRPLANE` it turns each cloud toward `uShadowCasterFacing`, the airplane position at render time, like the drawn clouds. Under `IMPOSTOR_LOWER_HEMISPHERE` the caster flips the light direction: an orthographic silhouette is the same from both ends of the light ray, so the view from below stands in for the view from the light. With `SHADOW_CASTER_COVERAGE`, [`scenery-shadow-caster-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/scenery-shadow-caster-fragment.glsl) writes the blended fractional coverage into a `RedFormat` target with `MaxEquation` blending and no depth test. It reads its own atlas: the drawn clouds' atlas has only frontal views, but the light can look at a cloud from any side. `CloudShadows` bakes a small lower-hemisphere atlas (`CLOUD_SHADOW_IMPOSTOR_FRAMES` `8 × 8` views of `32` px, `1024 × 256`, about `3` MB) without wood detail, because only its coverage is used; a cloud re-bake leaves it unchanged.
- **Blur:** a separable Gaussian ([`cloud-shadow-blur-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/cloud-shadow-blur-fragment.glsl)) blurs the map into a second target and back. The half-width is `softness / texel` (default `6` units, at most `8` texels).
- **When:** `shouldRenderCloudShadow()` renders the map again only when the airplane has moved more than `recenterShare` (`0.015`, about `16` units on desktop and `12` on mobile) of the radius, so shadows follow the clouds as they turn, when the light has turned past the scenery shadows' `lightThreshold`, when the cloud field has regenerated, or after `applySettings()`. Otherwise the last map and its matrix stay in use.
- **Receivers:** [`cloud-shadow-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/cloud-shadow-pars-fragment.glsl) provides `getCloudShadow(position)`, a single bilinear tap at the flat position. It fades to lit over `0.8`–`0.97` of the radius from the map center, and returns `1` early when `uCloudShadowStrength` is `0`. Terrain, near scenery meshes, and scenery impostors multiply it into `sceneryShadow` (see [Scenery Shadows](#scenery-shadows)), so it dims only the shadowing light's direct contribution. Clouds and the airplane receive no shadows.
- **Strength:** `uCloudShadowStrength = params.clouds.shadows.strength` (`0.55`) × the light-elevation fade from `selectShadowLight()`. It is `0` while `enabled` is off, before the first render, or without clouds. The glare takes `params.clouds.shadows.specular` (`1`) instead, through `uCloudShadowSpecular` (`getSpecularShadowScale()`, as for the [scenery shadows](#scenery-shadows)).
- **Frame order:** `World.tic()` calls `CloudShadows.update(sceneryShadows.light, plane)` after `Clouds.update()` and `SceneryShadows.update()`. It saves and restores the render target, clear color, and `autoClear`.
- **Cost:** the shadow atlas (about `3` MB) and two `R8` targets (`1024²` on desktop, about `2` MB), one draw of the cloud quads and two blur passes per render. With the default `360`-second day (`cycleDuration`), the sun crosses `lightThreshold` about every `0.2` s, so the map renders about five times per second while the cycle runs; at cruise speed travel alone triggers about three renders per second. Receivers pay one texture tap within the disk.
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getCloudShadowStats()` reports `enabled`, `strength`, `specularScale`, `radius`, `mapSize`, `center`, `renders`, and `renderMs` (CPU).
- **GUI:** **Clouds > Shadows** sets `enabled`, `strength`, **Glare removed** (`specular`), `softness`, and `radius` live.
- **Known limits:** shadows come from impostor silhouettes, not from the near meshes. With the sun below about 18°, clouds beyond the field radius can be missing near the edge of the map, where the edge fade already weakens shadows.

### Low-Sun Terrain Occlusion

The scenery shadow maps are flat: they fade in only above `0.05` rad of flat elevation and cannot exist once the sun sinks below the flat horizon, while the curvature still shows it above the edge (down to about `-0.2` rad at cruise height). Without them a low sun lit the sea in the very spots the islands hid from it, which showed as a bright glare at their base. [`src/sunOcclusion.js`](../src/sunOcclusion.js) hides the sun behind the relief while it is low; the pure rules and defaults are in [`src/sunOcclusionPolicy.js`](../src/sunOcclusionPolicy.js) (`createSunOcclusionSettings()`, `params.sunOcclusion`).

- **Height map:** a `RedFormat` half-float target (`1024²` over a square of half-size `2048` units on desktop, `512²` over `1536` on mobile: `4` and `6` units per texel) holds the terrain height above the drawn sea (`SEA_SURFACE_Y`, the `SUN_OCCLUSION_HEIGHT_BASE` define), so the cleared map is sea. The square moves `sunShift` (`0.25`) of its half-size toward the sun, where the marches go, and snaps to whole texels (`getSunOcclusionWindow()`).
- **Casters:** a pooled proxy per chunk that overlaps the square and rises above the sea (`chunkRisesAboveSea()`, from the flat sphere's height range, so open sea draws nothing) draws the chunk's shadow view in its own slot (`2`, [Terrain](TERRAIN.md#height-generation)) with a grid of `segments` (`32` on desktop, `16` on mobile, or the chunk's own when coarser). [`sun-occlusion-caster-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/sun-occlusion-caster-vertex.glsl) writes clip coordinates straight down (map x along world x, map y along world z, no camera), `DoubleSide`; [`sun-occlusion-caster-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sun-occlusion-caster-fragment.glsl) writes the height with `MaxEquation` blending, so overlapping chunks keep the highest.
- **When:** only while the strength is above `0`. `shouldRenderSunOcclusion()` renders at once when the square has moved `recenterShare` (`0.05`) of its half-size, and when the drawn chunk geometries change (by identity, like the [sea foam](#sea-foam)), at most every `minFrames` (`15`) frames, since chunk commits arrive in bursts. `applySettings()` forces a render.
- **Strength:** `getSunOcclusionStrength()` fades both shares with the sun's flat elevation, full below `elevation.full` (`0.15` rad) and gone above `elevation.end` (`0.3`), overlapping the shadow maps' fade-in so one takes over from the other. Both are `0` while `enabled` is off or the sun gives no light (night). `uSunOcclusionStrength` holds the glare's share (`specular`, `1`) and the diffuse light's (`diffuse`, `0`: the long shadows of a low sun, off by default).
- **March:** the terrain compiles [`sun-occlusion-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sun-occlusion-pars-fragment.glsl) under `SUN_OCCLUSION_STEPS` (`getSunOcclusionDefines()`: `12` on desktop, `6` on mobile). `getSunOcclusion()` runs once per pixel before the light loop, from the flat `vShadowPosition` and `distanceFromCamera`: `steps` samples of the map toward the sun (`uSunDirection`, never the moon), spaced quadratically from `firstStep` (`4`) to `reach` (`1600` on desktop, `1200` on mobile) and offset per pixel by interleaved gradient noise. It works in the world as drawn: every height is lowered by the curvature drop around `uCamera` (`getCurvatureDrop()`), so a sun below the flat horizon is still hidden from the sea near the eye and still reaches the distant sea that the curvature turns toward it. The clearance is the smallest slope of the ray over the relief; `smoothstep(±penumbra)` (`0.02`) turns it into a soft visibility, and the loop stops once the pixel is fully hidden. The ray starts from the higher of the drawn and the mapped surface, so the coarser map never shadows a pixel's own slope. `getSunOcclusionVisibility()` in the policy is its CPU twin, which the tests run.
- **Skips:** no march while both shares are `0`, outside the square, where the sun is below the pixel's local horizon or its face is turned away (no direct sun to hide), and, without diffuse occlusion, where the view ray is farther than `cone` (cosine `0.5`) from the sun: the glare of a rough surface needs a view toward the light, and at `60°` its Fresnel term is already about `0.07`.
- **Apply:** in the light loop the sun's direct light is scaled by the diffuse visibility and its glare by the specular one ([Curved-World Lighting](#curved-world-lighting)), on top of the scenery and cloud shadows.
- **Frame order:** `World.tic()` calls `SunOcclusion.update(chunks, CHUNK_SIZE, plane, dayNightState)` after `CloudShadows.update()`, so this frame's chunk commits are included. It saves and restores the render target, clear color, and `autoClear`; a placeholder mesh lets `compileAsync()` compile the caster at startup.
- **Cost:** the target (about `2` MB on desktop, `0.5` MB on mobile), one draw per caster chunk when the map renders (up to about `110` chunks of `2,048` triangles on desktop), and up to `steps + 1` bilinear taps per marching terrain pixel. With the [lens flare](#lensflareeffect), the glare masking, and the cloud translucency in the same build, `pnpm bench --compare HEAD` on the development machine measured the median frame within `+1%` at `1920 × 1080` (`--dpr 1`) with the default query and about `+0.4` ms (`+4%`) at `time=0.74`; at `3840 × 2160` the runs varied by several percent between sessions. Removing the march, the scene depth texture, or the flare alone did not remove the sunset difference, so it is at the edge of the bench's noise there.
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getSunOcclusionStats()` reports `enabled`, `specular`, `diffuse`, `radius`, `mapSize`, `steps`, `center`, `casterChunks`, `skippedSeaChunks`, `triangles`, `renders`, and `renderMs` (CPU).
- **GUI:** **Shadows > Low sun (terrain)** sets `enabled`, **Glare removed** (`specular`), **Long shadows** (`diffuse`), **Full below** and **Gone above**, **Penumbra**, **First step**, **Reach**, and **Glare cone** live; **Map radius** and **Shift toward sun** render the map again. The map size, grid segments, and steps are fixed at startup.
- **Known limits:** only the terrain occludes and receives: trees, clouds, and waves do not hide the sun from it, and the scenery keeps its glare. Thin ridges far away can fall between samples, and LOD `3` and coarser chunks are coarser than the map. Where the shadow maps also shadow, the optional diffuse darkening stacks with them.

### Aurora

An aurora borealis can light the night over an ice region. [`src/aurora.js`](../src/aurora.js) draws it; [`src/auroraPolicy.js`](../src/auroraPolicy.js) decides when and where, without Three.js. Settings are `params.aurora` (`createAuroraSettings()`); their defaults, tuned in the GUI, are `AURORA_DEFAULTS`.

- **Event:** auroras are random (`Math.random`, injected for the tests), never tied to the world seed. One aurora exists at a time; while it does, nothing rolls.
  - **Rolls:** `stepAurora()` reads the ice field under the airplane (`getIceValue()` with the live biome offset) with hysteresis: inside from `ice.enter` (`0.005`), outside below `-ice.exit` (`-0.03`). Entering the ice rolls `chance` (`0.4`; `?aurora=<0..1>` overrides it). So does every dawn spent in the ice, for the next night. Leaving and entering again rolls again.
  - **Region:** a successful roll scans the region under the airplane (`findIceRegion()`): a four-connected flood fill of the ice field on a world-aligned grid of `region.cellSize` (`300`) cells, up to `maxCells` (`6000`). The cells give the centroid, the main axis (principal components), and a mask with an empty border, blurred `region.blur` times. It takes about `1` ms once warm (about `5` ms the first time) for the large region of `?seed=ice194` (`1225` cells), once per aurora.
  - **Time:** `getAuroraNightVisibility()` opens a window in palette time ([Day/Night Cycle](#daynight-cycle)): it rises from `night.start` (`0.74`, the end of sunset) to `night.full` (`0.8`, dusk) and falls from `night.fade` (`0.17`) to `night.end` (`0.235`, before sunrise). A new aurora also fades in over `fadeIn` (`8`) seconds from its creation, so entering the ice at night never pops it in; one created by day has finished by the evening. The visibility is the product of both.
  - **Removal:** the first step whose window is closed after an open one (dawn) removes the aurora. Leaving its region does not: it stays anchored over the region and sinks below the curved horizon behind the airplane. A paused cycle never reaches dawn; dragging the time of day from night to day does. A seed change removes it (`Aurora.reset()`), and a biome distribution change scans its region again from where it was found (`refreshRegion()`), removing it when no ice is left there.
- **Curtain window:** a finite aurora would hide below the horizon from most of a large region (a curtain top about `900` units up sinks below the curved edge about `3` km away). The curtains are therefore a world-anchored field drawn in a window around the airplane, like the clouds. In the event's frame (origin the region's centroid, `axis`, and the side vector `(-axis.z, axis.x)`), ribbon `j` runs along the axis at `j × area.spacing` sideways. The mesh has `ribbons` slots × `columns` segments × `rows`, from `getAuroraLayout()`: the columns span `area.radius` both ways in steps of `area.step`, and the slots reach every ribbon whose sideways displacements (meander, folds, sway, jitter) can bring it inside the radius. Every frame `getAuroraWindow()` snaps the first column to a whole step and the first slot to a whole ribbon around the airplane (`uAuroraWindow`), and every hash and noise in the shaders takes the ribbon index and the distance along the axis, so shifting the window never moves a curtain. The defaults (radius `4900`, spacing `220`, step `40`, `3` rows) give `52 × 247 × 3` cells, `51,584` vertices; mobile draws a radius of `3500` in steps of `60`, `40 × 119 × 3` cells and `19,200` vertices.
- **Vertex ([`aurora.vert.glsl`](../src/shaders/aurora.vert.glsl)):** `position` holds the column, the height share, and the slot.
  1. The ribbon's ground line: a per-ribbon `jitter` (a share of the spacing), a broad `meander`, and finer `fold`s, both drifting with the event's time `uAuroraTime`.
  2. The visibility: the region mask `uAuroraMask` (a linear `R8` `DataTexture`, bounds in `uAuroraMaskBounds`) at the ground point, times the `presence` noise that splits ribbons into arcs, times a fade from `area.fadeStart` of the radius to the radius. Where it is `0` the curtain's height is `0`, so empty stretches and the window's edges are degenerate triangles that cost no fragments.
  3. The curtain: `getCurvatureBend()` drops the ground point onto the curved world and tilts its normal; the vertex rises along that normal from `area.altitude` (`280`, about the cloud tops) by `area.height` (`600`), which varies by up to `heightVariation` along the ribbon. The `sway` bends the top sideways, growing with the square of the height share.
  4. `vAuroraFacing`: how squarely the curtain faces the eye, from its direction half a column ahead.
- **Fragment ([`aurora.frag.glsl`](../src/shaders/aurora.frag.glsl)):** two `snoise()` octaves of vertical `rays` along the ribbon brighten the curtain and push its frayed top higher; a base (`look.bottomSoftness`) and a top (`topSoftness`) bound it, with `falloff` dimming the top; `pulse` brightness waves run along it. The color blends the three `params.aurora.colors` (sRGB, converted by `Color.set()`) from base to `middleStop` to top: dark green, teal, and blue by default. It fades within `look.horizonFade` (`0.06` rad) above the curved edge (`asin(direction.y) + uHorizonDipAngle`, as in the sky; `DayNight`'s dip reaches it through `dayNightState.horizonDip`) and as a curtain turns edge-on (`edgeFade`), so thin sheets do not flicker. The result times `look.intensity` and `uAuroraVisibility` adds onto the frame; with ACES the composer's float buffers keep values above `1` until tone mapping.
- **Material:** a `ShaderMaterial` like the sky, unlit and fog-free: `transparent`, `AdditiveBlending`, no depth write, depth test on (terrain, scenery, and clouds hide it), `DoubleSide`, frustum culling disabled, `renderOrder` `-1` (before the trails, which stay on top). Its uniforms are a copy of the shared object (`uCamera` and `uCurvature` bend it with the world) plus its own. The mesh is visible until its first update, so `World.precompileShaders()` compiles it; afterwards it is visible only while the aurora is.
- **Cost:** one draw, whose overlapping additive curtains cost fill rate. On a night flight over `?seed=ice194` (`pnpm bench -- --query "seed=ice194&time=0.9&aurora=1"` against `aurora=0`), the defaults measured `4.55` ms mean against `4.31` at `1280 × 800` (`--width 1280 --height 800 --dpr 1`). At the default `3840 × 2160` runs varied between sessions: `35.3` against `33.4` ms in one pair, `29.1` against `21.4` in another, on the development machine.
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getAuroraStats()` ([Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md#stats-api)).
- **GUI:** **Sky > Aurora** ([Development](DEVELOPMENT.md#debugging-notes)).
- **Known limits:** the region scan runs on the main thread, once per aurora. The curtains are zero-thickness sheets; a curtain exactly edge-on fades out instead of looking thick. The aurora does not light the ground.

### Sea Foam

The sea ripples that follow the coast's depth contours also circle the sea rocks, which are instances and do not change the terrain's `height`. [`src/seaFoam.js`](../src/seaFoam.js) owns the pass; the pure rules and defaults are in [`src/seaFoamPolicy.js`](../src/seaFoamPolicy.js) (`createSeaFoamSettings()`, `params.seaFoam`).

- **Map:** an `R8` target (`1024²` over a square of half-size `600` units on desktop, `512²` over `360` on mobile, about one unit per texel) stores, for each flat world point around the airplane, its closeness to the nearest sea rock: `1` inside a rock's footprint circle, falling linearly to `0` at `reach` (`12`) units outside it. The square's center is snapped to whole texels (`getSeaFoamWindow()`).
- **Casters:** a pooled proxy per chunk whose scenery holds sea rocks and can reach the square (`chunkIntersectsSeaFoamWindow()`, with a `40`-unit margin for footprints and satellites across chunk edges) reuses the chunk's impostor `InstancedBufferGeometry`. Each geometry's sea rock count is computed once (`countSeaFoamInstances()`), so chunks without sea rocks are skipped. [`sea-foam-caster-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-foam-caster-vertex.glsl) writes clip coordinates itself (map x along world x, map y along world z, no camera): a horizontal square around each base, `footprint radius + reach` wide, or a collapsed one for types with a zero radius. [`sea-foam-caster-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-foam-caster-fragment.glsl) writes the closeness with `MaxEquation` blending and no depth test, so every texel keeps the nearest rock.
- **Footprints:** `uSeaFoamRadius[IMPOSTOR_TYPE_COUNT]` holds each type's radius at scale `1`: for `SEA_FOAM_TYPES` (the sea rock), the farthest LOD 0 source vertex from the vertical axis within the lowest `40%` of its height (`getFootprintRadius()`, about `2.5` units); `0` for every other type.
- **When:** `shouldRenderSeaFoam()` renders the map again when the airplane has moved `recenterShare` (`0.02`) of the radius (`12` units on desktop), when the list of drawn chunk geometries changes (a chunk with sea rocks streams in or out, or its scenery is replaced), or after `applySettings()`. Otherwise the map stays world-locked, so ripples never slide.
- **Receiver:** `getSeaFoamHeight(height, xz)` in [`sea-ripple-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/sea-ripple-pars-fragment.glsl) fuses the raw height with the rocks into the one sea height the ripples are drawn on. It returns the raw height on land (`height ≥ 0`), outside the square, or without a rock within reach. Otherwise it converts the closeness to the distance `d` from the rock's edge and clamps the raw height between `edgeDepth − slope × d` and `edgeDepth + slope × d` (`-5` and `0.4` per unit), with a smooth maximum and minimum over `blend` (`1.5`) height units. This double cone holds the sea at `edgeDepth` next to the rock and lets it return to the raw height farther out:
  - Over deep water the rock raises the sea like an islet: the coast's lines circle it from its edge out to about `6` units and move toward it, as they move toward the shore.
  - Over shallow water, where the coast draws no lines, it lowers the sea, so a rock near the beach still gets one or two lines hugging it, spreading outward.
  - The lines are contours of one height, so where the rocks' and the coast's meet they bend into each other instead of crossing.
  - The rocks' weight fades over the outer quarter of the reach, where the cone (`-8.6` to `-1.4`) is already wider than the lines and the blend, and between `85%` and `97%` of the square's half-size, so no seam shows. A test checks that the defaults keep this margin.
- **Strength:** `uSeaFoamShape` is `(reach, edgeDepth, slope, strength)` and `uSeaFoamBlend` the blend; the strength (`0`–`1`, the rocks' weight in the fused height) is `0` before the first render, while `enabled` is off, or without scenery (no `SeaFoam`).
- **Frame order:** `World.tic()` calls `SeaFoam.update(chunks, CHUNK_SIZE, plane)` right after `SceneryImpostors.update()`, so this frame's scenery commits are included. It saves and restores the render target, clear color, and `autoClear`. A placeholder mesh lets `compileAsync()` compile the caster program at startup; it is hidden from the first render on.
- **Cost:** the target (`1` MB on desktop, `0.25` MB on mobile), one draw per chunk holding sea rocks when the map renders (about every `12` units of travel), and one bilinear tap per sea fragment within the square that is not on land. Placement adds the rocks and their satellites to the scenery instances.
- **Stats:** `window.__INFINITE_WORLD__.getSeaFoamStats()` reports `enabled`, `strength`, `radius`, `mapSize`, `center`, `casterChunks`, `renders`, and `renderMs` (CPU).
- **GUI:** **Sea surface > Foam around rocks** sets `enabled`, `strength`, `reach`, edge depth, depth per unit (`slope`), blend with the coast, and `radius` live; the map size is fixed at startup. The foam lines' own look is in the same folder ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)).
- **Known limits:** footprints are circles, so ripples round off the outline of a group of stones. Rocks beyond the square, which reach into impostor range, keep no ripples. Over shallow water a rock's lines spread outward, the opposite of the coast's, because the rock lowers the sea there.

### Airplane Propeller

Both toy airplanes have the propeller fused into their single mesh, so `Propeller` ([`src/propeller.js`](../src/propeller.js)) turns it in the vertex shader of the model's own material ([`propeller-pars-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/propeller-pars-vertex.glsl)). The per-model values come from the model's `AIRPLANE_MODELS` entry ([`src/airplaneModels.js`](../src/airplaneModels.js)).

- **Selection:** at startup, `getPropellerMask()` ([`src/propellerMask.js`](../src/propellerMask.js)) groups the mesh into index-connected components and marks every vertex of each component that reaches beyond `propeller.minZ` (`0.445` model units for the biplane, `0.28` for the monoplane). The mask goes in a per-vertex `propeller` attribute, an unnormalized `Uint8` with values `0` and `1`. Each component is one UV chart, and each triangle belongs to exactly one, so turning whole charts never stretches a triangle. A coordinate threshold alone does not work: near the blade roots the blades and the cowl overlap in position (see [Assets](ASSETS.md#airplane)).
- **Rotation:** after `beginnormal_vertex` and `begin_vertex`, marked vertices and their normals turn about the axis through `uPropellerAxis` (`propeller.axis`), parallel to +Z in model space, by `uPropellerAngle`. The normal map uses screen-space derivatives (no tangent attribute), so it follows the rotated surface.
- **Speed:** `Propeller.update()`, called from `Plane.update()`, advances the angle by `params.propeller.speed` turns per second (default `4`), so the propeller stops during the debug flight pause. The **Airplane > Propeller speed** GUI control (`?gui=1`, `0`–`20`) changes it live. With two blades, speeds near half the frame rate (`30` turns/s at 60 fps) strobe and look still.
- **Cowl plugs:** `propeller.plugs` lists dark (`#3b291c`, roughness `1`) discs and ring sectors, children of the model centered on the axis, that close openings the turning propeller reveals. Only the monoplane needs them: a ring sector behind its fused blade root and a disc behind the spinner. They sit inside the cowl, so at rest the airplane looks unchanged. The biplane has none.
- **Shadow:** the `SceneryShadows` airplane caster does not apply the rotation, so the propeller casts a still shadow.

### Trails

- [`trail-project-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/trail-project-vertex.glsl) preserves Three.js projection and forwards the trail attributes (UV, widths, traveled distance, bank), declared in [`trail-pars-vertex.glsl`](../src/shaders/trail-pars-vertex.glsl).
- Trail cutout and color come from [`trail-pars-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/trail-pars-fragment.glsl) and [`trail-color-fragment.glsl`](../src/shaders/trail-color-fragment.glsl), patched in by `WingTrails`, from UV, the stored width, and edge noise driven by traveled distance. Speed and curvature are captured per pose on the CPU rather than applied to the entire ribbon at render time.
- Most of the ribbon never shows a stripe, so the cost is cut twice. `WingTrails.refresh()` draws only the segments that touch a row with a non-zero width, so level flight at cruise speed draws nothing. The fragment shader then discards pixels with zero width, or outside the widest stripe the edge noise and oscillation can reach (both noise sums stay within `±1`, bounded with a `1.05` margin), before it evaluates any of its five `snoise()` calls. `fwidth()` runs before that discard.
- The trail's white core is multiplied by `uTrailTint`, which `Plane.setDayNight()` sets (through `WingTrails.setTint()`) from the cycle's `trailTint` keyframes. The unlit ribbon turns pink at dawn (`[1, 0.62, 0.7]`) and orange at sunset (`[1, 0.6, 0.35]`), stays white by day, and turns blue at night (`[0.45, 0.6, 1]`). The tints stay bright so the trail remains readable against the sky. The dark outline is unaffected.

### Noise Tile

[`src/noiseTexture.js`](../src/noiseTexture.js) builds, once per page on the main thread (about `50` ms), a tileable noise texture that the purely visual noise samples instead of evaluating `snoise()` per pixel; its rules and the generator are the pure [`src/noiseTexturePolicy.js`](../src/noiseTexturePolicy.js).

- **Content:** four independent fields, one per channel of a `512²` RGBA8 tile (about `1.3` MB with mipmaps): periodic gradient (Perlin) noise with quintic fade on a lattice of `32` cells per side that wraps, so the tile repeats seamlessly (`RepeatWrapping`, `16` texels per cell, smooth under bilinear filtering). Each channel has its own unit gradients, hashed from the lattice point (`cellRandom()`). The values are scaled by `NOISE_GAIN` (`2.2`) to the spread of `snoise()` (standard deviation about `0.47`, so thresholds tuned for it keep their look; about `4%` of the tile clamps at `±1`) and stored as `n × 0.5 + 0.5`.
- **Scale:** `NOISE_UNIT_CELLS` (`1.65`) lattice cells per unit of the shaders' noise coordinates matches the mean slope of `snoise()` over its spread, so a feature keeps its size per unit. The tile repeats every `32 / 1.65 ≈ 19.4` noise units: about `1.6` km and `600` units for the two octaves of the terrain color patches, `1.9` and `19` km for the biome variation, `270` units for the foam line dashes, and `550` for the crest flicker. The fields and their shapes differ from the former `snoise()`, as decided for this change; the character stays.
- **Sampling:** `sampleNoiseTile(p, footprint)` in [`noise-texture-pars.glsl`](../src/shaders/noise-texture-pars.glsl) (guarded, so several chunks can include it) returns the four fields at `p`, in noise units, mapped back to about `[-1, 1]`. The callers sit in non-uniform control flow, where implicit derivatives are undefined, so it reads `textureLod()` at the mip level of `footprint`, the pixel's size in noise units (the ground pixel size times the caller's frequency): a scalar, which does not over-blur the noise at a grazing angle as the larger of two derivatives would. Far away the fields average toward `0` instead of aliasing. One sample serves two fields at the same coordinates (the crest fray).
- **Defines and uniform:** `NOISE_SHADER_DEFINES` (`NOISE_TILE_SIZE`, `NOISE_TILE_UV_SCALE`) on the terrain material; `uNoiseTexture`, a shared uniform ([`src/sharedUniforms.js`](../src/sharedUniforms.js)) kept for the page. With it the terrain fragment uses `13` of the `16` texture units WebGL2 guarantees (six normal maps, two shadow cascades, the cloud shadow, sea foam, and low-sun maps, three's `dfgLUT`, and the tile).
- **Users:** the terrain color patches and the biome variation ([Terrain](#terrain)), the foam line dashes, the crest lines' flicker and fray ([Sea Surface](#sea-surface)), and the sea ice's edge wobble ([Sea Ice](#sea-ice)). The fields tied to the CPU twins never use it: the climate, ice, and deep ocean fields, the rocky coast mask, and the sea state must match [`src/biome.js`](../src/biome.js), [`src/coast.js`](../src/coast.js), or the boats. The breaking foam's lace (off by default), the aurora, and the trails keep `snoise()`.

## Uniform Update Timing

- `World.tic()` updates `uTime` and `uCamera` once per frame after `plane.update()` and before rendering.
- `createSharedUniforms()` ([`src/sharedUniforms.js`](../src/sharedUniforms.js)) creates `uAtmosphere` in the shared uniform object; `DayNight` writes it in its constructor (before the first compile) and on every frame after `uCamera`.

- `createSharedUniforms()` creates `uCurvature` from `CURVATURE` in `src/worldConstants.js`, the same constant used by the CPU curvature helpers. Do not recreate it per chunk: materials that compile before the first chunk exists read it immediately.
- The biome uniforms (`uBiomeClimate`, `uBiomeIce`, `uBiomeOcean`), the terrain palettes (`uTerrainColors`, `uTerrainVariations`, `uTerrainStyles`, `uSeaColors`, `uSeaAbyssDepth`), and the sea ice (`uSeaIceShape`, `uSeaIceEdge`, `uSeaIceColors`) are written from `params` by `updateBiomeUniforms()`, `updateTerrainPaletteUniforms()`, and `updateSeaIceUniforms()` when `createSharedUniforms()` builds them, and again, in place, by the `?gui=1` **Biomes** folders.
- `uBiomeOffset` and `uBiomeOceanOffset` (the deep ocean's noise offset, summed from it on the CPU) are derived from the world seed and written by `updateBiomeOffsetUniforms()`. `ChunkManager.setSeed()` rewrites them in place when the `?gui=1` **World** folder changes the seed. Before `World.init()`, `World.applyWorldSeed()` writes them directly.

- `uTerrainNormalMaps`, `uTerrainNormalScale`, `uTerrainNormalStrength`, and `uTerrainNormalRotation` (the `(cos, sin)` of each layer's rotation, computed on the CPU) are arrays indexed by `TERRAIN_BANDS` in [`src/terrainNormals.js`](../src/terrainNormals.js). `createSharedUniforms()` adds them from `params.terrainNormals`; every `uTerrainNormalMaps` entry starts as `FLAT_TERRAIN_NORMAL` and `loadTerrainNormalTextures()` swaps in each KTX2 map once it is transcoded. The **Terrain > Normal maps** GUI calls `updateTerrainNormalUniforms()`, which writes the values in place. `uTerrainNormalStrength` is a `vec2` per layer, and its Y includes the texture's `invertGreen` sign.
- The impostor material adds its own atlas uniforms (`uImpostorAlbedo`, `uImpostorNormal`, `uImpostorPaint`, and `uImpostorTypes` with per-type frame radius and center height) next to the shared uniforms at compile time.
- `SceneryImpostors` owns the scenery palette uniforms (`createSceneryPaletteUniforms()`) and the per-type wood detail array `uSceneryDetail`. `applyPalette()` rewrites the palette in place (`setSceneryPaletteUniforms()`) on every palette or rock color change in the **Biomes** folders; `applyDetail()` rewrites the detail array before each re-bake.
- `Plane.update()` writes `uAcceleration` and updates the trail buffer before the frame renders.
- `Plane.update()` ends with `WingTrails.record()`, which pushes the current pose into the history, and `WingTrails.refresh()`, which copies `params.trails` into stable trail-uniform wrappers and rebuilds the ribbon from the history. Changing a GUI slider affects already emitted sections as well as new ones. During the debug flight pause only `Plane.refreshTrails()` runs, so tuning stays live without extending the trail.
- `World.tic()` passes `max(uAcceleration, params.speedEffect)` to `PostProcessing.setSpeedEffect()` and the `DayNight` state to `PostProcessing.setSun()` immediately before `PostProcessing.render()`; `LensFlareEffect.update()` writes its uniforms from the live settings during the render.
- `createSharedUniforms()` adds `createSceneryShadowUniforms(params.shadows)`. `SceneryShadows.update()` writes `uSceneryShadowStrength`, `uSceneryShadowSpecular`, and `uSceneryShadowLight` every frame, and each cascade's `uSceneryShadowMatrices`, `uSceneryShadowCascades`, and `uSceneryShadowDepthScale` entries only when that cascade renders. `applySettings()` writes `uSceneryShadowFade`, `uSceneryShadowSoftness`, and `uSceneryShadowBias`. `uSceneryShadowMaps` holds the cascade depth textures from construction.
- `createSharedUniforms()` adds `createCloudShadowUniforms()`. `CloudShadows` assigns `uCloudShadowMap` at construction, writes `uCloudShadowStrength` and `uCloudShadowSpecular` every frame, and writes `uCloudShadowMatrix` and `uCloudShadowWindow` when the map renders.
- `createSharedUniforms()` adds `uCoastSandShade` from `params.coastSand.shade` (written by the GUI), `uCoastRockNoise` and `uCoastRockEdge` from `params.coast.mask` (`updateCoastMaskUniforms()`, also called by the **Rocky coast mask** GUI), and `createSeaFoamUniforms()`. `SeaFoam` assigns `uSeaFoamMap` at construction (and again when the map size changes), writes `uSeaFoamShape` and `uSeaFoamBlend` in `applySettings()` and the strength in `update()`, and writes `uSeaFoamWindow` when the map renders.
- `createSharedUniforms()` adds `createSunOcclusionUniforms()`. `SunOcclusion` assigns `uSunOcclusionMap` at construction, writes `uSunOcclusionShape` and `uSunOcclusionStrength` every frame, and writes `uSunOcclusionWindow` when the map renders.
- The moving sea's uniforms (`uSeaWaves`, `uSeaWaveSizes`, `uSeaWaveShape`, `uSeaRegions`, `uSeaSurface`, `uSeaRippleDetail`, `uSeaRipples`, `uSeaCrestLines`, `uSeaCrestWaves`, `uSeaCrestFlicker`, `uSeaCrestFray`, `uSeaBreaking`, `uSeaWaveLagCos`, `uSeaWaveLagSin`, `uSeaMinimum`, `uSeaCrestFoam`, `uSeaFoamColor`, and the `uSeaFoamLine*` arrays) are written from `params.seaSurface` by `updateSeaSurfaceUniforms()` when `createSharedUniforms()` builds them, and again, in place, by the **Sea surface** GUI folder. It keeps every `smoothstep()` edge pair ordered. Their motion comes from `uTime` alone, so nothing writes them per frame.
- The cloud field keeps a copy of the shared uniform object with its own `uSceneryMeshRange`, plus `uImpostorFarFade`, `uSceneryAmbientScale`, its translucency (`uCloudTranslucency`, `uCloudTranslucencyColor`, `uCloudTranslucencyMoon`), its variation uniforms, its palette uniforms (one cloud palette for every type), and its wood detail uniforms. `Clouds.applyAppearance()`, `applyPalette()`, `applyMeshSettings()`, and `rebake()` write them in place.
- `Aurora` keeps a copy of the shared uniform object with its own `uAurora*` uniforms and `uHorizonDipAngle`. `applySettings()` writes the shape, motion, and look uniforms (on creation and from the **Sky > Aurora** GUI); `applyEvent()` writes the frame, mask, and seed when the event changes; `update()` writes `uAuroraWindow`, `uAuroraTime`, `uAuroraVisibility`, and `uHorizonDipAngle` every frame the aurora is visible.
- Shader callbacks merge custom uniform entries with Three.js-generated uniforms at compilation time.

Do not replace the shared uniform wrapper objects each frame. Update their `.value` fields so compiled materials retain the same references.

## Materials And Textures

- Terrain assigns one normal map per layer in `TERRAIN_NORMAL_LAYERS` ([`src/terrainNormals.js`](../src/terrainNormals.js)). Each entry has a `texture` (a `TERRAIN_NORMAL_TEXTURES` key), a `scale` (tile size in world units), a `strength`, and a `rotation` in degrees, counter-clockwise seen from above. The default rotations are arbitrary hard-coded values (`50`, `71`, `137`, `204`, `256`, `318` from sea to snow). Consecutive layers differ by at least `15°`, even modulo `90°`, so the patterns of neighbouring bands never line up; keep that gap when changing them. The sea uses the `ribbedCorduroy` map with an `85`-unit tile and strength `2`, whose ribs snake with the [sea surface](#sea-surface)'s ripples. `curlyTeddy` and `fabric` (`normal`, green channel inverted) stay available but no layer uses them. The five land bands use the fabric maps (hessian, ribbed corduroy, waffle piqué, dirty carpet, fabric pattern), assigned arbitrarily, with per-band tiles and strengths in `TERRAIN_NORMAL_LAYERS`. Swap the `texture` keys to reassign them.
- The material's `normalMap` is `FLAT_TERRAIN_NORMAL`, a 1 × 1 texture. It only enables Three's tangent-space path; the terrain shader samples `uTerrainNormalMaps` instead, and `normalScale` is unused.
- The terrain material is a module-level shared instance. Its shader hooks and mutable properties therefore affect every chunk.
- `SceneryImpostors` creates the scenery impostor material once and hands it to `ChunkManager` for every chunk. It owns the atlas render target. Its wireframe twin shares those uniforms without owning them. Chunks dispose only their own scenery geometry.
- `Clouds` owns its own atlas, impostor material, wireframe twin, and near meshes, and disposes them in `dispose()`. It replaces (and disposes) its impostor geometry on every regeneration.
- The boat's color map belongs to its sources and lives as long as the page. `SceneryMeshes` disposes its per-level `map` materials, never the map.

See [Assets](ASSETS.md) for load paths, transforms, and licensing.

## Safe Change Checklist

1. Identify every JavaScript owner that injects the edited GLSL file.
2. Confirm required uniforms, varyings, and attributes are declared in both shader stages that use them.
3. Preserve the original Three.js include when the replacement depends on built-in declarations or behavior.
4. Run `pnpm build` to validate imports and bundling.
5. Load the scene and check the browser console for shader compile or link errors. `World.precompileShaders()` compiles hidden objects too (near scenery and cloud meshes, the aurora, the cloud shadow passes, the sea foam and low-sun height map casters), so their errors appear at startup rather than when they first come into range.
6. Inspect terrain, water, scenery impostors, clouds, boats, and trails as applicable.
7. For post-processing changes, hold the effect with `?gui=1` and the **Speed effect > speedEffect** slider (same smoothstep mapping as a real boost) or **Speed effect > preview** (raw intensity), and check the center, edges, and corners, and that the edges ease back to the idle level after a boost (`getPostProcessingStats().speedEffectIntensity` about `0.126`).
8. For the lens flare, turn on **Lens flare > Debug > Show occlusion taps** and check that the taps sit on the sun's disc, turn red behind terrain, trees, clouds, and the airplane, and that the flare fades at the screen edges and when the sun sets.
9. Move far enough to exercise distance fades, curvature, new chunks, and multiple LODs.
10. Repeat at a narrow/mobile viewport because geometry density and camera settings differ.

## Open Questions

- Should shader contracts receive automated compile checks against the installed Three.js version?
- Should module-level materials be replaced with explicitly owned or cached variants?
- Should curvature and atmosphere calculations share one documented distance convention? The atmosphere color is now tied to the day/night cycle, but its distance ranges (`700`→`200` terrain, `700`→`400` impostors; clouds skip it) remain independent of the fog range.
- Should the palettes and light intensities be retuned for ACES Filmic? They were tuned without tone mapping, and the night is darker; the higher night exposure (`1.3`) only partly compensates.
- What visual baseline should be used for regression screenshots?
